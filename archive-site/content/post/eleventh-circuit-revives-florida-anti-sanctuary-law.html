Hailey Konnath ( Listen to article) for Law360 reports on the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit ruling that vacated a district court order invalidating portions of a Florida law restricting local "sanctuary" immigration policies, finding that the immigrant advocacy groups that challenged the measure lacked standing.

The three-judge panel said the groups lack standing to bring their suit against Republican Governor Ron DeSantis and Attorney General Ashley Moody because they have not established a "cognizable injury" stemming from the law, S.B. 168.  Chief Circuit Judge William H. Pryor Jr. wrote on behalf of the panel.

The Eleventh Circuit vacated the district court's September 2021 ruling and remanded the case with instructions "to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction."

The city of South Miami and several organizations, including the Florida Immigrant Coalition, had argued that the original version of the measure was drafted by recognized "anti-immigrant hate groups" that had recruited Florida lawmakers to push their agenda.

U.S. District Judge Beth Bloom agreed, finding that the city and groups had met their burden of demonstrating racial discrimination was a motivating factor behind the passage of S.B. 168.
