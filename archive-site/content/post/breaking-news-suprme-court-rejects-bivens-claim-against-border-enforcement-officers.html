The U.S. Supreme Court just released its decision in Egbert v. Boule, which raised the issue of constitutional claims under Bivens against border enforcement officers.  The SCOTUSBlog.com page on the case is at Egbert. v. Boule. 

Justice Clarence Thomas wrote the opinion for the Court, which rejected Bivens claims for excessive force and First Amendment retaliation.  Justice Sotomayor, joined by Justices Breyer and Kagan, filed an opinion concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part.

This case involves immigration enforcement activity at a bed and breakfast (the Smuggler's Inn) at the U.S./Canada border.  Sounds fun to teach!

Several cases, including Hernandez v. Mesa (2019) (rejecting a claim based on a cross border shooting resulting in a death) involving Bivens claims against border enforcement officers have been presented to the Supreme Court in recent years.

UPDATE (June 9):  Howard Wasserman for SCOTUSBlog analyzes the decision and its restriction, but not elimination, of Bivens claims.  Mark Joseph Stern for Slate criticizes the civil rights implications of the decision in Egbert.
