The Migrant Family Separation Crisis: The Multifaceted Approach to End the Practice, Obtain Redress, and Prevent its Return by Mariela Olivares

Abstract

Adding to my previous work about family detention and family separation, this Article examines the multi-faceted advocacy against policies of family detention and family separation that target migrant families. Part I begins with the history of the detention of families and children and the family separation policies. Expounding on the history in contemporary times, this part shines a light on the first Trump Administration’s egregious actions, leading to the separation of children from their parents and caregivers. These policies directly resulted in migrant death and irreparable harm and trauma. This part then focuses on the effects of family separation under the Biden Administration, including the work of the Executive Family Reunification Task Force (Task Force), which was dedicated to investigating the separations, reuniting families, and strategizing so that separations do not occur again. Perhaps unsurprisingly, in 2025, the work continues to reunify separated families—albeit without the Task Force, which Trump disbanded in 2025.

Part II discusses the litigation efforts that moved the public outcry to the courthouse. Section A explores how advocates for migrant families have argued constitutional violations and, with increasing vehemence, have filed civil suits to, ultimately, make the government pay for its unconstitutional, torts and human rights violations. And as Section B describes, the illegal and unconstitutional governmental actions against migrant families are part of an entrenched history that oppresses certain communities of immigrants due to their intersectional identities of non-citizen, perceived criminal, and racial or ethnic minority. Contemporarily, it is unsurprising that immigrant communities have been targeted and demonized for cheap political points. Once again, and as I have argued in past articles, the identity politics that malign immigrants as criminals who steal American jobs, homes, and even eat their pets, is fueled by the powerful intersectionality of immigrant identity—that is, the immigrant is a criminal non-human un-deserving of compassion or help. In the current politically vitriolic environment, it is increasingly possible that family separation could return as anti-immigrant narratives continue to fuel law and policy. 

Part III elucidates the other important avenue of resistance—that of social outcry and protest. The practice of family separation finally received the spotlight needed for the country to understand what the government does in its name. This part discusses the multifaceted efforts to halt the inhumane practices of separation and family detention. Frighteningly, in the 2024 presidential campaign season, Republican presidential candidate Trump expressed support for the family separation system and has not ruled out imposing it again in his second presidency, as quoted above. Similarly, Vice President JD Vance, when specifically asked, did not rule out the possibility of separating migrant families in the Trump-Vance Administration. With the November 2024 election behind us, the Article briefly concludes with a prediction of the efforts to resurrect the Family Separation Policy, based in part on the discourse of the 2024 campaign season about migration and immigrants. The Article asserts in conclusion that, although family separation polices ended, as the new Trump Administration asserts its immigration enforcement priorities, it is essential that lawyers and activists fight for law and policies that permanently prevent separations and continue to implement practices to reunify and mend families.
