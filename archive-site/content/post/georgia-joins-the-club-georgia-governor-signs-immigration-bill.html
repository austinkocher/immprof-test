Georgia has followed Florida, Texas, and other states with an immigration enforcement law.  Suzanne Gamboa for NBC News.  Georgia Governor Brian Kemp yesterday signed a new immigration law that he said responded to the death of "Laken Riley would improve public safety, but opponents said it would erode trust between immigrant and Latino communities and law enforcement."  

Georgia HB 1105 requires, among other things, sheriff’s offices to coordinate with federal officials on people in their custody who may be in the country unlawfully, or face losing funding if they do not. 

Kemp, said the bill “became one of our top priorities following the senseless death of Laken Riley at the hands of someone in this country illegally who had already been arrested even after crossing the border,” The Associated Press reported.
