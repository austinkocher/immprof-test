Canada has received a larger share of immigrants than most other destination countries in the global north over the past several decades. Yet these high rates of immigration have been backed by a national consensus that has largely prevented the rise of anti-immigrant sentiment seen in many other destination countries.

The latest report from the Migration Policy’s Transatlantic Council on Migration, What’s So Special about Canada? Understanding the Resilience of Immigration and Multiculturalism, explores the evolution of Canada’s apparently unique attitude toward immigration and diversity. The report offers a snapshot of Canadian polling on immigration and discusses the matrix of social policies, institutions, and institutional practices that have driven this positive consensus.

While some share of Canada’s success is attributable to factors that are impossible to emulate—including its immigration history and geographic location—certain policy choices underpinning the Canadian immigration and integration model may offer lessons for policymakers in other countries: taking a whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach to immigration and integration policy, developing neutral narratives around immigration policy, implementing thoughtful selection policies and maintaining diverse flows, and matching commitments with action, altering course as needed.

The report is the fifth in a Transatlantic Council series that examines the drivers of anxiety that often surround immigration and explores the conditions under which these can be addressed. Find the rest of the series here. The series will conclude with a Council Statement that seeks to analyze how rising anxiety about immigration overlaps with other concerns, becoming activated in certain national contexts to disrupt social cohesion and fuel extremist political views.
