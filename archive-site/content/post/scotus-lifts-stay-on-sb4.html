Today, SCOTUS lifted its administrative stay on SB4.

Here's a reminder of where we've been so far:

  • On February 29, U.S. District Court Judge David Alan Ezra enjoined the state of Texas from enforcing SB4. (Here is the 114-page order.)
  • On March 2, the 5th Circuit granted a stay of the trial court's decision, but put its ruling on hold for seven days, allowing time for the Biden administration to go to the Supreme Court. (Here is that opinion.)
  • The Biden Administration then "rushed" to the Supreme Court to review the 5th Circuit stay.  (Here is their brief.)
  • On March 4, Justice Alito "administratively stayed" the 5th Circuit decision, blocking SB4 from going into effect until (for now, at least) March 13 and requesting briefing from Texas due March 11. (Here is the administrative stay.) He later extended that deadline.
  • Today (March 19), the Supreme Court vacated Alito's stays.

Justice Barrett, in concurring with the decision, wrote an opinion (joined by Justice Kavanaugh) that focused on procedure. She noted that the 5th Circuit's decision was only an administrative stay and that the court was still evaluating whether to issue a stay pending appeal. "So far as I know, this Court has never reviewed the decision of a court of appeals to enter—or not enter—an administrative stay. I would not get into the business." She concluded by stating that "If a decision does not issue soon [from the Fifth Circuit], the applicants may return to this Court."

Justice Kagan dissented and wrote her own opinion on the matter. She found the majority's focus on procedure unconvincing: " I do not think the Fifth Circuit’s use of an administrative stay, rather than a stay pending appeal, should matter."

Justice Sotomayor also dissented and wrote a much lengthier opinion that Justice Jackson joined. She argues that by lifting the stay, "The Court gives a green light to a law that will upend the longstanding federal-state balance of power and sow chaos, when the only court to consider the law concluded that it is likely unconstitutional."
