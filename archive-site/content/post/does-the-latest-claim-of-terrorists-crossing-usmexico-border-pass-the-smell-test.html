--- title: "Does the Latest Claim of Terrorists Crossing the U.S./Mexico Border Pass the Smell Test?" date: 2014-10-11T06:41:27 url: "/post/does-the-latest-claim-of-terrorists-crossing-usmexico-border-pass-the-smell-test/" aliases: ["/immigration/2014/10/does-the-latest-claim-of-terrorists-crossing-usmexico-border-pass-the-smell-test.html"] type: post year: "2014" category: "Current Affairs" category_slug: "current-affairs" byline: "Immigration Prof" ---

A report by Samantha Ehlinger of the Tribune News Service seems to be reaching to try to substantiate claims that dangerous persons with "terrorist ties" recently crossed the U.S./Mexico border.  Since 2001, such claims have been invoked regularly to justify escalating immigration enforcement efforts along the border. 

Ehlinger reprts that "[a] series of statements this week from Obama administration officials have left a murky picture about whether four people caught last month crossing the United States border from Mexico had ties to terrorist groups. U.S. Rep. Jason Chaffetz, R-Utah, raised the issue last month when he said he had heard that individuals with terrorist ties to the Middle East had been caught crossing the border. Other Republican members of Congress have made similar claims since."

The story quotes Marsha Catron, Department of Homeland Security's press secretary, as stating that the claim that persons with ties to the Islamic State, also known as ISIL, had crossed the border was "categorically false." and that "DHS continues to have no credible intelligence to suggest terrorist organizations are actively plotting to cross the southwest border."  Secretary of Homeland Security Jeh Johnson layer told an audience that four people had in fact been apprehended, but that their "supposed link" to terrorism was "a claim by the individuals themselves" that they were members of the Kurdistan Workers' Party, which Johnson described as "an organization that is actually fighting against ISIL and defended Kurdish territory in Iraq." This  raised more questions, according to the Ehlnger story, because the Kurdistan Workers' Party,  known by its Kurdish initials as the PKK, has been on the State Department's list of foreign terrorist organizations since 1997. State Department categorization of organizations as "terrorist" have long been challenged as overbroad. 

What we seem to have here is the State Department's overbroad categorization of allegedly terrorist organizations with the reality of the risk posed by the PKK members who were apprehended.

All in all, the claims that the U.S./Mexico border is a sieve through which terrorists enter the United States remains to be proven and, to this point, does not seem to be supported by the facts.
