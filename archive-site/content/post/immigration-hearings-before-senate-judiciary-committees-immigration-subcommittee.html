Earlier this week, Senator Alex Padilla (D-CA), Chairman of the Senate Judiciary Committee's Immigration Subcommittee, presided over a hearing about the challenges faced by immigrants and the need to “overhaul and modernize the system.”   Here is a news report on the hearing.

The son of Mexican immigrants, Senator Padilla, with others, introduced the Resolving Extended Limbo for Immigrant Employees and Families (RELIEF) Act, which, among other things, would eliminate the visa backlog.

Chairman Padilla and Ranking Member John Cornyn heard testimony from three individuals: Athulya Rajakumar, Professor Stephen H. Legomsky, and Lynden D. Melmed.

Here is Steve Legomsky's testimony.  He concluded as follows:

"It has been 32 years since Congress last meaningfully updated our system of legal immigration. Since then, massive backlogs have accumulated. Millions of immigrants who qualify for admission – and the American family members and businesses who sponsor them -- continue to wait for years. Statutory caps too low to accommodate present-day flows, combined with
bureaucratic delays occasioned by inadequate resources and recently exacerbated by the Covid travel restrictions, account for these backlogs. "

Rajakumar, a graduate of the University of Texas and a member of Improve the Dream, spoke about her experiences being an immigrant from India who aged out of her green card application after turning 21.

ILynden D. Melmed, former Chief Counsel, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, focused on the barriers to skills-based migrations.  He argued that the lengthy backlog is the central problem in the “U.S. high-skilled immigration system.”
