Babcia & Pop Pop

 

One-in-four of all home health aides nationwide are immigrants, according to Forbes.

But the new "public charge" regs may affect the ability of noncitizen aides to continue in this line of work. Why? Because the pay is so low for this type of care, many aides rely on public supports like food stamps and Medicare. The new regs mean that accessing those services now bears the threat of being found a "public charge." This affects nonimmigrants in the U.S. and nonimmigrants who would otherwise come to the U.S. to engage in care-giving work.

Forbes points out the essential paradox here: "the cost of long-term care is so high that most Americans already can’t afford it." Many Americans are making do with aides who are noncitizens. Those noncitizens are able to do the work because of the public support they've been previously allowed to rely on. Take that public support away, and the aides will leave. That could make the market for home health care workers shrink and cause prices rise to even more unaffordable heights.

Perhaps you're one of the lucky readers who hasn't yet had to figure out how to care for a relative in need. But, wake-up call here, EVERYONE IS JUST GETTING OLDER. That means, if this hasn't yet been a problem for you, it surely will be in the not-so-distant future.

We are facing, as Forbes calls it, a "caregiving catastrophe." I, for one, am terrified.
