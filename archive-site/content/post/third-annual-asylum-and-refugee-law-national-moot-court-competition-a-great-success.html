As previously announced here, UC Davis School of Law hosted the Third Annual Asylum and Refugee Law National Moot Court Competition yesterday at the fedearl courthouse in Sacramento, California. This is the only immigration moot court competition on the west coast and the only asylum and refugee law competition in the nation. All arguments took place in the Federal District Courthouse in Sacramento.

I attended the final rounds and was most impressed by the advocates on the two finalists, teams from NYU and UNLV.  The problem involved an asylum-seeker from the fictitious country of Pacifica who claimed to have a well-founded fear of persecution on account of political opinion.  Besides claiming that the applicant had failed to establish the requisite fear, the government claimed that he was barred from relief because he had persecuted others.  An interesting problem indeed!

The distinguished bench for the final round included Judge Carlos Bea (U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit) (Bea himself immigrated here from Cuba), Judge William Shubb (Eastern District of California), Immigration Judge (Los Angeles) Mimi Tsankov (appearing in her personal capacity), and Immigration Judge (San Francisco) Robert Yergin (appearing in his personal capacity).

After the final rounds, the judges provided very helpful and constructive feedback to the advocates.  A reception and awards ceremony followed the competition in the courthouse rotunda.  Who won the finals?  NYU in a cliffhanger!
