Remember a few years back when Arizona, Alabama, Georgia, South Carolina, and other states passed state laws designed to spur on immigration enforcement.  In Arizona v. United States (2012), the Supreme Court found that critical provisions of Arizona's SB 1070 was preempted by federal law.

The state of Florida is at it again.  Here is a summary of recent developments from WUSF:

"The Florida Senate late Thursday approved a measure aimed at boosting immigration enforcement that critics maintain could have far-reaching implications for migrants in the state, such as people seeking asylum from Communist regimes and children awaiting reunification with their families.

The proposal . . . is a top priority of Gov. Ron DeSantis. It targets transportation companies that bring undocumented immigrants into the state and would expand a 2019 law that sought to ban so-called `sanctuary cities.'

The bill coincides with an effort by the DeSantis administration to shutter shelters that provide housing and other services to unaccompanied children whose immigration or refugee status is being processed after they enter the country. The children are placed in the shelters by the federal Office of Refugee Resettlement. . . . 

. . . Senate bill sponsor Aaron Bean, R-Fernandina Beach, said during debate Thursday night that the legislation (SB 1808) is designed to send a message to President Joe Biden and to Congress to take action on immigration." bold added).

Readers might recall that California voters in 1994 passed the state anti-immigrant measure Proposition 187 to "send a message" to the U.S. government on the need for immigration enforcement.  The message was sent and received, leading to immigration and welfare reform in 1996, increased border enforcement operations, and more.
