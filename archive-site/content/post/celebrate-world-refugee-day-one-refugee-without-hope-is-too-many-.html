June 20 is World Refugee Day.  For years, many countries and regions have been holding their own Refugee Days and even Weeks. One of the most widespread is Africa Refugee Day, which is celebrated on 20 June in several countries.

The United Nations General Assembly, on 4 December 2000, adopted resolution 55/76 where it noted that 2001 marked the 50th anniversary of the 1951 Convention relating to the Status of Refugees, and that the Organization of African Unity (OAU) had agreed to have International Refugee Day coincide with Africa Refugee Day on 20 June. The General Assembly therefore decided that, from 2001, 20 June would be celebrated as World Refugee Day.

This year the United Nations High Commissioner on Refugees (UNHCR), in its 60th year, will mark World Refugee Day with a rich and varied programme of events in locations worldwide and the launch of a new global awareness campaign. UNHCR will start rolling out the multimedia "One" campaign next week. Over the next six months it will increase awareness about the forcibly displaced and stateless by telling their powerful personal stories. The campaign will carry the message that "One Refugee Without Hope is too Many." Every day, millions of refugees face murder, rape and terror. We believe even 1 is too many.

The United Nations High Commissioner on Refugees has put together the following materials to raise awareness about the human stories of today's refugees:

Video: 1 Life 1 Story:  UNHCR is talking to refugees and other forcibly displaced people around the world about what forced them from their homes and how that experience has shaped their lives. Here are their stories.

Nowhere People: documenting the plight of the world's stateless people View the photos of Greg Constantine, who has documented the stories of stateless people around the world.

 60 Years 60 Lives: The Magnum Photo project 60 refugee portraits (6 by Magnum photographers, 54 others) from the 30 (asylum) countries and six decades to symbolise 60 years of Europe as a safe haven for refugees. The refugees have been chosen carefully to enable us to tell the story of the most numerous refugee groups who arrived in Europe since 1951, including refugees from other European countries.

A UNHCR report released today reveals deep imbalance in international support for the world's forcibly displaced, with a full four-fifths of the world's refugees being hosted by developing countries – and at a time of rising anti-refugee sentiment in many industrialized ones.
