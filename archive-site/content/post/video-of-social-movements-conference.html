This is a video of the recently held symposium (May 13, 2015) at the UCLA Chicano Studies Research Center (CSRC), focusing on the themes of grassroots social movements, Latino immigrants, and municipal laws.   Organized by CSRC, the panelists (all of whom had an active role in this social movement) provide historical information and critical analysis on Latino immigrant gardeners and their efforts to defend themselves against a leaf-blower ban. From Victor Narro's insightful comments on immigrant civic engagement in Los Angeles to law professor Scott Cummings' brilliant legal analysis of this case and related ones to Adrian Alvarez' nuanced and humorous reflections on organizing Latino immigrants in the informal economy, this educational event represents an excellent resource for scholars, students, activists, planners, immigrants, policy makers, etc. on "Organizing Latino Immigrants in the Informal Economy: The Successful Case of the Association of Latin American Gardeners of Los Angeles." 

To learn more about this historic social movement, see Alvvaro Huerta and Alfonso Morales.   “The Formation of a Grassroots Movement: The Association of Latin American Gardeners of Los Angeles Challenges City Hall.” Aztlán: A Journal of Chicano Studies 39 (2): 65–93 (2014).
