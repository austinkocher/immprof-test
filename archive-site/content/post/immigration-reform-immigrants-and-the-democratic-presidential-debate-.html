MSNBC reports on how the Democratic candidates painted a stark picture for Latino voters at the first debate last night: If they want to see a pro-immigrant president in the White House, they will have to run far away from the Republican Party.

When given a chance to challenge Republican presidential front-runner Donald Trump, candidates took it.

Former Maryland Gov. Martin O’Malley, who has a detailed immigration platform, called Trump the “carnival barker of the Republican Party.”

Hillary Clinton criticized candidates who “demonized hardworking immigrants” and “insulted” them.

The Democratic candidates expressed similar views on immigration issues. The question posed in the debate was not whether candidates support a pathway to citizenship for the estimated 11 million undocumented immigrants, but whether those people qualify for the Affordable Care Act and instate college tuition.

“On this stage you didn’t hear anyone denigrate women. You didn’t hear anyone make racist comments about new Americans. You didn’t hear anyone speak ill because of a religious belief,” O’Malley said in his closing statements.

Immigration is a point of some vulnerability for Bernie Sanders. He came out against a comprehensive immigration reform bill in 2007. Though he now says he supports an immigration overhaul, he has expressed concerns that undocumented immigrants entering the workforce would depress wages. He explained his vote against the 2007 immigration reform package:  “I voted against that piece of legislation because it had guest-worker provisions in it which the Southern Poverty Law Center talked about being semi-slavery.”

O’Malley  had championed a fairly consistent, pro-immigrant stance throughout his two terms as governor of Maryland. It comes in contrast with Clinton, who has "evolved" over the years. She very notably stumbled during a debate back in 2007 when asked if she supported granting drivers’ licenses to undocumented immigrants. When pressed on whether she agreed with O’Malley in extending the Affordable Care Act to undocumented immigrants and their children, Clinton said she was open to the idea of allowing people to buy into the exchanges, but would limit some of the subsidies.

Clinton, Sanders and O’Malley have all said that they would go even further than Obama, and include protections to the undocumented parents of DREAMers, immigrants brought to the U.S. as children.

The tone of the Democratic debate contrasts sharply with the Republican debates.  This headline from Fusion put it nicely yet subtly: "Dems address immigration as if it were a serious issue involving real people."
