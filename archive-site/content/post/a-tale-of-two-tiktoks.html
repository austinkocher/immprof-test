I'll confess, A Tale of Two TikToks is probably a misleading post title. I won't be discussing two particular TikToks. Or even two particular TikTok accounts. Rather, I want to talk about news articles highlighting two very different, if both problematic, trends on immigration TikTok.

First, an April 2021 Mother Jones piece is making rounds again. It highlights how immigration lawyers have used the social media app TikTok to drum up business. The article questions whether these TikTok'ing immigration attorneys are ethically representing the likelihood of being able to help noncitizens #arreglarsinsalir (“fix without leaving”) their immigration status so as to avoid the INA § 212 unauthorized-stay bars. (Props to Kit of the past who covered this MJ article in 2021 with the post title To TikTok or Not to TikTok, That Is The Question.)

Second, the BBC this week has coverage of a very different immigration TikTok trend: using the app to recruit clients to human smuggling networks. This trend also uses hashtags to gain visibility: #AmericanDream. In this case, the videos aren't from lawyers but smugglers promising safe guidance into the United States alongside disinformation about Title 42 restrictions on entry. These smugglers, the article reports, are part of a "highly sophisticated, multi-billion dollar illegal industry that has taken advantage of confusion over changes in US immigration policies."

The BBC article is interesting apart from this TikTok hook: The news agency interviewed Ed Calderon, a former Mexican police officer "who specialises in the border's underworld." Calderon offered these facts and figures about currently smuggling prices:

Mr Calderon estimated that even the most basic packages - which he described as a "let's see if we can get there" trip - would start at between $5,000 and $8,000 for a family of four starting in Mexico.

Current and former law enforcement sources in the US and Mexico estimated that typical prices are now around $15,000, rising to between $50,000 and $60,000 for many migrants coming from outside of Latin America. 

At those prices, a little TikTok'ing to drum up business can go a long way.
