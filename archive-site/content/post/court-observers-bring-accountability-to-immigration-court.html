Legal observers are individuals who attend public demonstrations, protests and other activities where there is a potential for conflict between the public and law enforcement. The purpose of legal observers is to monitor, record, and report on any unlawful or improper behaviour. Legal observers intend for this record-keeping to keep law enforcement accountable for their actions. A specific variant is the immigration court observer.

Nonprofits and immigrants rights organizations nationwide have mobilized court observers to attend asylum hearings to shed light on the immigration court system. Qualifications for observers are minimal—one needs only a valid government photo ID and the ability to observe in silence and take legible notes, since recording devices aren’t allowed -- and law students are excellent candidates. Volunteer observers can plug into different programs to share their observations, as well as discuss the process with family and friends or post their findings on social media. Collectively, this information can be used to highlight judges or courts that are particularly unfriendly to asylum seekers. It can also empower advocates pushing for systemic reform of the 50 immigration courts nationwide deciding the fate of every asylum seeker.

Some of the court observer programs mentioned in the story include - please post more in comments, if you know of them:

  • The Advocates for Human Rights (Minneapolis, Minnesota)
  • Innovation Law Lab (Atlanta, Georgia)
  • Al Otro Lado (Los Angeles, California)
  • AILA Colorado mobilizes law students to observe immigration court, providing standardized forms to report on hearings (Denver, Colorado)
  • Immigration Court Observation Project (NYC, law student project)
  • Stand with Immigrants Court Observation (national campaign)
  • An Immigration Court Watch browser-based app by Ariel Prado scheduled to launch in mid-August that uses standardized forms to document immigration judges’ conduct, the hearing outcome, and other factors. The forms are uploaded onto the app, which will read responses, ask follow-up questions, and store the information in a centralized location.
