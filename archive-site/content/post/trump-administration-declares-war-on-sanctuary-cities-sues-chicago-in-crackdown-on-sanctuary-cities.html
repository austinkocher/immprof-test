New Attorney General Pam Bondi has hit the ground running.  Sophia Tareen for the Associated Press reports that the Trump administration yesterday filed suit against Chicago alleging that ‘sanctuary’ laws in the nation’s third-largest city “thwart” federal efforts to enforce immigration laws. 

The lawsuit also names the state of Illinois.  It is the latest in the effort to crack down on jurisdictions that limit cooperation between U.S. immigration agents and local police. It follows the U.S. government’s threats of criminal charges and funding cuts to sanctuary cities.

New Attorney General Pam Bondi ordered an end to Department of Justice grants for jurisdictions that “unlawfully interfere with federal law enforcement.”

The first Trump administration faced setbacks in the courts in seeking to cut funding to sanctuary jurisdictions and challenging sanctuary laws, including California's.  As the AP story states, "[t]he administration has already made clear that legal precedent won’t necessarily stand in its way. Lawyers say that simply filing lawsuits can intimidate jurisdictions to back away from their sanctuary laws."

According to Bloomberg, Bondi also announced the Justice Department will ensure that so-called “sanctuary” jurisdictions cannot access any federal funding, and paused distribution of “all funds” until completion of a review.
