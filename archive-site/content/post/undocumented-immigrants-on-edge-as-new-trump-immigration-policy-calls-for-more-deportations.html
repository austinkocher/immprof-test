The Trump administration's expansion of expedited removal, to allow for quick removals of immigrants apprehended anywhere in the United States, has stricken fear in, and questions from, immigrants.  Alan Gomez in USA Today reports on how an immigration attorney in Colorado, Aaron Hall,  is still trying to figure out what exactly to tell his clients about expedited removal. For the past 15 years, expedited removal has been limit to immigrants apprehended near the border.

Under the expanded expedited removal, Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents can question, arrest, detain and deport undocumented immigrants who have been here for less than two years that they encounter anywhere in the country.

The question arise on how immigrants can protect themselves from expedited removal.  Should immigrants carry all their papers proving their legal status every time they leave the house? Should undocumented immigrants carry paperwork showing they've been in the country longer than two years? Should all Latinx persons approached by ICE remain silent and demand to speak with an attorney?

"It's really tricky," Hall said. The only certainty is that "people who have legal immigration status, or have pending applications, or even U.S. citizens, will be put into expedited removal proceedings, and some will be deported before they’re able to make their case."
