While many progressives have lamented the Supreme Court's overruling of the Chevron doctrine in Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo, I have not been so sure.  Why?  The Board of Immigration Appeals' historically has interpreted ambiguities in the immigration statute against immigrants and the courts regularly invoke Chevron to defer to the BIA's interpretations.  Could the end of Chevron -- and courts being the final arbiters on statutory interpretation -- be all that bad for immigrants? 

Before today, I had never used Chat GPT.  But I ran my query about Chevron and immigration law through the program.  Here is one of the two responses that II received; the other was not that different:

"The end of Chevron deference could have mixed implications for immigrants and immigration policy. Chevron deference refers to the judicial principle that courts should defer to administrative agencies' interpretations of statutes they administer, as long as those interpretations are reasonable.

If Chevron deference is curtailed or eliminated, courts might take a more active role in interpreting immigration laws and policies. This could lead to:

  1. Increased Scrutiny: Courts might be less inclined to uphold broad interpretations by immigration agencies, potentially resulting in stricter enforcement of immigration laws.

  2. Greater Legal Clarity: On the other hand, clearer judicial interpretations could provide more consistent standards for immigration cases, which might benefit some immigrants seeking stability in their legal status.

  3. Challenges to Agency Policies: Immigrant advocacy groups could have more opportunities to challenge specific agency policies or practices that they view as unjust or overly harsh.

Overall, the impact on immigrants would depend on the specific legal interpretations and policies that emerge in the absence of Chevron deference."

Although I might be a bit more optimistic, the Chat GPT response is not bad!
