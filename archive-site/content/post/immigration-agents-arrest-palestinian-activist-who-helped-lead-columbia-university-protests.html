Jake Offenhartz for the Associated Press reports that U.S. immigration authorities yesterday arrested a Palestinian activist who leader in Columbia University’s protests against Israel. "Mahmoud Khalil, a graduate student at Columbia until this past December, was inside his university-owned apartment Saturday night when several Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents entered and took him into custody, his attorney, Amy Greer, told The Associated Press."

Expect this high profile case to generate litigation.  It already is generating lots of chatter on the internet.  The Supreme Court (Reno v. American-Arab Antidiscrimination Committee (1999)) has set a high barrier to any claim of selective enforcement of the immigration laws.

ICE spokeswoman Tricia McLaughlin says Khalil was arrested pursuant to President Trump’s executive order on antisemitism and that he had “led activities aligned to Hamas, a designated terrorist organization.” NPR’s Adrian Florido says conservative lawmakers have labelled pro-Palestinian protesters as pro-Hamas, pro-terrorist and antisemitic. Students have rejected these claims and have said the protests were for peace in Gaza. Student activists are denouncing Khalil’s arrest as an attempt to use deportation to stifle free speech.

 

UPDATE (Mar. 11):  NPR updates us on the story.  A federal judge has ordered the government not to deport Mahmoud Khalil. Khalil, a lawful permanent U.S. resident and recent Columbia University graduate, participated in protests against the Israel-Gaza war. He is being detained in Louisiana.  Khalil’s attorneys filed a habeas corpus petition in federal court. His lawyers also say that his transfer to Louisiana undermines his ability to access legal counsel and family. Trump posted on social media that Khalil’s arrest was the first of many. Khalil’s attorneys say he is being used as an example by the Trump administration to stifle lawful dissent.
