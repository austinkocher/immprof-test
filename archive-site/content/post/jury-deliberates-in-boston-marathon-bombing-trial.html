 ImmigrationProf has not reported in a while about developments in the case of Dzokhar Tsarnaev and the Boston Marathon bombing.

The latest news out of Boston is that jurors in the trial of Dzhokhar Tsarnaev are set to resume deliberations for a second day in the first phase of his federal death penalty trial. The jury of seven women and five men has began deliberations on 30 federal charges against Tsarnaev.

During the trial, Tsarnaev's lawyer admitted he participated in the deadly attack, but claimed that  his dead brother, Tamerlan, was the mastermind. If Tsaranev is convicted, the jury will hear evidence in a second phase of the trial to decide whether he should be sentenced to death or life in prison.  Many court watchers believe that the question of guilt is a foregone conclusion and that the only real question is whether the jury will recommend the death penalty for Tsarnaev, who is not yet 22 years old.

Tamerlan was born in the Kalmyk Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic, and Dzhokhar was born in Kyrgyzstan. Their father, Anzor Tsarnaev, is a Chechen, and their mother, Zubeidat Tsarnaeva, is an Avar.  The brothers were born into a Muslim family. Their father was a traditional Muslim who reportedly shunned religious extremism.  As children, Tamerlan and Dzhokhar lived in Tokmok, Kyrgyzstan. In 2001, the family moved to Makhachkala, Dagestan, in the Russian Federation. In 2002, the Tsarnaev parents and Dzhokhar went to the United States on a 90-day tourist visa. Anzor Tsarnaev applied for asylum, citing fears of deadly persecution due to his ties to Chechnya.  Tamerlan arrived in the U.S. around two years later. The parents received asylum in the United States and their four children received "derivative asylum status".

UPDATE (APRIL 8 3 p.m): The jury convicted Tsarneav on all 30 counts of the indictment and the case now will move on to the death penalty phase.
