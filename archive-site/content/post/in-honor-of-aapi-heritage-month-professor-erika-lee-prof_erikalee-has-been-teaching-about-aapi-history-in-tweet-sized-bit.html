In honor of AAPI Heritage month, Professor Erika Lee (@Prof_ErikaLee) has been teaching about AAPI history in tweet-sized bites. Her #AAPIHM thread has 17 parts so far.

  1. We've been here a long time. Our history is tied to the Europeans' colonization of both Asia and the Americas.
  2. Asian American women have a long history of perserverence. In 1610, Mirrha-Catarina de San Juan was brough as a slave from India to Mexico.
  3. AAPI history is the history of white supremacy. The 1790 Naturalization Act allowed only free "white" persons to become naturalized citizens. This meant generations of Asian immigrants (and Blacks and Indigenous people) couldn't come and had little political power.
  4. Asian American history is tied to the history of slavery in the Americas. In the 1800s, South Asians were sent to the W. Indies and Chinese went to Cuba and Peru as indentured laborers.
  5. Asian Americans are part of global diaspiras. In times of crisis, we've organized to help. South Asians throughout the diaspora are focusing on COVID relief in India and across South Asia.
  6. In 1882, President Arthur signed the Chinese Exclusion Act, the first federal law that singled out a group for immigration exclusion based on their race and class.
  7. U.S. has long history of commodifying and exploiting Asian women. In 1834, Afong Moy was brought from Guangzhou to NYC by 2 businessmen who exhibited her as "the Chinese lady." She was the first known Chinese woman in the U.S.
  8. Chinese immigrants really did build the railroad. But there's so much more. Visit the Chinese railroad workers in North America Project.
  9. Our communities were built by moms working, caring, and fighting for us. Happy Mother's Day.
  10. AAPI history is the history of US colonialism. In 1893, the U.S. overthrew Queen Lili'uokalani in Hawaii... and made a U.S. territory. Lili'uokalani spent the rest of her life fighting for the rights of Native Hawaiians.
  11. In 1989, Wong Kim Ark continued the work of Black activists and took his civil rights battle to the Supreme Court to affirm birthright citizenship for all persons born in the U.S. 
  12. Racial justice is for all of us. Hate crime law passed after Ahmaud Arbery murder to be used in Atlanta shootings case.
  13. Kala Bagai came to the US in 1915 through Angel Island. She faced brutal discrimination, but she persevered. Now there's a Berkeley street named after her.
  14. Latest from AAPIData: Indian Americans are now the largest AA community out of 23 M in the US. There's great diversity w/in the AA community, but experiences with anti-Asian racism -- past and present -- unite us.
  15. Smithsonian APA and American History Museum are collecting our shared past; a mission that is more important than ever before.
  16. The #AsianAmericansPBS series is streamling online for free this month! Through amazing family and community stories, the series shows how systemic racism, community resilience, and solidarity have brought us to where we are today.
  17. Before the #MuslimBan, there was the "Asiatic Barried Zone." Put in place in 1917, it targeted S Asians and barried immigrations from almost all of Asia (500M people).

Happy heritage month!
