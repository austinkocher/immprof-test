Do you ever wonder why facts often are so misrepresented in the discussion of immigration in the United States? Matthew Yglesias on Slate has a thoughtful piece on how Americans consistently and dramatically overestimate the number of immigrants in the country. Yglesia's post links to a Wall Street Journal article ("Americans Stumble on Issues of Big Math") that reports that"

"Americans vastly overestimate the percentage of fellow residents who are foreign-born, by more than a factor of two, and the percentage who are in the country illegally, by a factor of six or seven. They overestimate spending on foreign aid by a factor of 25, according to a 2010 survey."

It appears that numbers and statistics on public policy issues often don't get properly absorbed by many Americans. That means that those of us seeking to educate the nation about immigration have our work cut out for us.
