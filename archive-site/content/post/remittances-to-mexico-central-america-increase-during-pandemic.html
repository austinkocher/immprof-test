Folks worried that the COVID pandemic would reduce remittances sent by immigrants in the United States to their home countries. Yet, as the LAT reports today, remittances to Mexico actually increased.

What kind of numbers are we talking about? According to the LAT:

In March [2019], remittances hit their highest level since record-keeping began in 1995, surging 36% to $4 billion. July [2019] was the third-highest level on record, central bank data showed.

The LAT argues that there are several different factors accounting for this increase including that many immigrants (i) work in essential jobs and thus have been able to continue to earn during the economic downturn and (ii) have increased remittances even when earning less because family abroad is even harder hit by COVID/the economic downturn.
