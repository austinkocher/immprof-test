For obvious reasons, the global pandemic is dominating the news.  But the beat goes on.  As the end of the U.S. Supreme Court Term nears, many await the decision in the case involving the future of the Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA) policy.  It is an anxious time for hundreds of thousand of DACA recipients, including many college students. 

Appreciating the anxiety in the air for many students, the President of the University of Nevada, Las Vegas, Marta Meana, yesterday sent out the following e-mail to the student body and university community:

 

Dear Students and University Community,

In the midst of the COVID-19 pandemic that has affected every facet of all our daily lives, we know that members of our university community are awaiting the U.S. Supreme Court’s ruling on the future of Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA), which is expected before June. As you may be aware, the federal government rescinded the DACA program in September 2017, resulting in litigation that has worked its way through the courts.

The Supreme Court’s decision will have a direct effect on members of our university community and their families. DACA provides legal work authorization and protection from deportation for an estimated 13,000 Nevadans and enables approximately half a million undocumented students to enroll in colleges and universities across the U.S.

UNLV stands together with other colleges and universities that support DACA students. Undocumented and DACA students are an integral part of our university community. We believe they should be able to pursue their educational goals and seek opportunities to improve their lives without fearing for their safety or their future. We continue to work with the Nevada Congressional Delegation and through the Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration to advocate for a Congressional solution to this issue.  

The Presidents’ Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration and the UNLV Immigration Clinic strongly encourage all DACA recipients to renew their DACA status as soon as possible, and prior to the ruling by the U.S. Supreme Court. This is especially critical if their status is set to expire in the next 12-18 months. Students and employees may set up an appointment with the UNLV Immigration Clinic to receive free assistance with completing their renewal forms. Students requiring renewal fee assistance can also apply to the Supporting Our Students (S.O.S.) Emergency Student Relief Fund.

Below you will find upcoming virtual events and available resources to further support undocumented members of our university community.

DACA-related events this week:

NSHE Virtual DACA Town Hall with Chancellor Reilly 
The Nevada System of Higher Education will be hosting a town hall with Chancellor Reilly at 4 p.m. tomorrow, Tuesday, April 28, to discuss concerns around the uncertainty of the Supreme Court DACA decision and its impacts on immigrant students and staff. Learn more and submit your questions at tinyurl.com/NSHEDACA. 

Roots & Resilience: Healing for Undocumented Communities
The Undocumented Student Program and Student Diversity & Social Justice (SDSJ) will be hosting a healing workshop at noon on Friday, May 1, with information about immigration-related stress, as well as supportive resources and strategies for coping. Learn more and join the conversation through SDSJ’s Instagram account (@unlvsdsj).
 
University Resources:

Emergency Relief
The Supporting Our Students (S.O.S.) Emergency Student Relief Fund provides both one-time (per academic year) funding when assistance is needed with unexpected emergencies as well as sustained financial coaching to develop budgetary literacy skills. All current students are eligible to apply for S.O.S. funding in instances including, but not limited to, funding DACA renewals. 
 
University Legal Services – UNLV Immigration Clinic 
University Legal Services offers free legal advice and representation to UNLV and CSN students, employees, and their family members. The clinic also provides free DACA renewal form assistance to any person residing in Nevada. Due to the COVID-19 pandemic, the clinic is currently completing DACA renewals over phone or video. To schedule an appointment, please schedule online or email at immigrationclinic@unlv.nevada.edu. 
 
Campus Support
Mariana Sarmiento is a Resource Coordinator in the Office of Diversity Initiatives, where she provides assistance for undocumented students and DACA recipients by coordinating campus and community resources such as legal assistance, basic needs, mental health support, and more. To receive assistance, please email Mariana at mariana.sarmiento@unlv.edu.  
 
Counseling & Psychological Services
Students in need of emotional and mental health support during this time are encouraged to contact UNLV Counseling & Psychological Services (CAPS). CAPS is currently offering telehealth visits (services available by phone) until further notice.  
 
We will continue to closely watch this issue and we will provide you with updates as this situation unfolds. We stand united as one and in support of our entire UNLV family. 

Warm regards,
 
Marta Meana, Ph.D.
President
