The history of the prisoners’ rights movement in the U.S. is rich with legal nuance and intrigue. Writ Writer, an award-winning documentary, portrays a chapter in that story that played out against the backdrop of the civil rights movement. In it, a Texas prisoner named Fred Arispe Cruz struggled to defeat in court the state's prohibition against inmates assisting one another with lawsuits. Fighting on behalf of inmates too poor or uneducated to effectively petition state courts, Cruz became a leader for prison reform. Backed by lawyer Frances Jalet, he confronted the prison system touted as "best in the nation," fought for due process, and laid the legal groundwork that would make reform possible.
