The issue of birthright citizenship in the United States has been much in the news lately, touched off by Donald Trump’s suggestion that as president he would seek to repeal the grant of U.S. citizenship to babies born in the United States to unauthorized immigrant parents.

While much of the debate has focused on the mechanics of how one would repeal this right enshrined in the Constitution, far less attention has been paid to the results of such a policy, not just for those who would be directly affected, but for the broader society.

Ina new commentary, MPI President Michael Fix examines what denial of citizenship to people born on U.S. soil would create: A self-perpetuating class that would be excluded from social membership for generations. In an earlier analysis with researchers at Pennsylvania State University, MPI found that repeal of birthright citizenship for those born to two unauthorized
immigrant parents would expand the unauthorized population by 4.7 million
people by 2050.

As Fix makes clear, creation of a permanent underclass, where disadvantage is
inherited, would be contrary to the national interest.
