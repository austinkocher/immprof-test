The backlogged (and compromised) immigration court reform system needs reform.  Jill Family adds to the calls for reform in this piece in the Yale Journal on Regulation.  She writes:

"If anything, the establishment of more independent immigration judges would promote sovereignty by reinforcing the United States’ foundational rule of law values.  The current dependent framework led to the abuses of the Trump administration.  The goal should be a system where immigration judges decide cases based on the law and not based on fear of reprisal from a boss who does not like the state of the law.  Attorney General Sessions welcomed a new group of immigration judges by telling them that “the vast majority of the current asylum claims are not valid.”  Such politicization only denigrates the rule of law."
