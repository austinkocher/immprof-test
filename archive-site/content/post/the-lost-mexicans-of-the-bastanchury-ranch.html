Last week, ImmigrationProf posted a story on the "repatriation" to Mexico of a U.S. citizen from Chicago. Unfortunately, it was not an isolated incident but hundreds of thousands of persons of Mexican ancestry were removed from teh country.  Gustavo Arellano writes that "[T]he Mexicans who lived on the Bastanchury Ranch in the early 1930s were subject to one of the largest mass deportations in Orange County history, with hundreds of them in late March of 1933—single men and families, Mexican nationals and American citizens—thrown onto trains bound for Mexico, carrying with them only the clothes on their backs and whatever belongings they could lug along. Almost overnight, a vibrant community vanished, the homes of former residents demolished, its memory bulldozed into wealthy neighborhoods, the few surviving scraps locked in university archives or in the recollections of those few families that escaped exile. Eighty years ago this spring, officials deported hundreds of legal residents whose only crime was being Mexican during the Great Depression—and Orange County has tried to forget ever since."
