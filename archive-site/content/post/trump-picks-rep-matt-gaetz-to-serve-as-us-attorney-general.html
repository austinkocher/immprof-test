The U.S. Attorney General is the nation's chief law enforcement officer and heads the Department of Justice, which houses the immigration court machinery (including the Executive Office for Immigration Review).  In President Trump's first term, Attorney Generals Jeff Sessions and William Barr intervened in a number of Board of Immigration Appeals matters to encourage case closures and narrow eligibility for asylum.

CNN reported yesterday that President-elect Donald Trump has selected Congressman Matt Gaetz of Florida, a staunch conservative and loyalist to President Trump, to serve as his attorney general.  The announcement has generated a fair amount of controversy:

"The selection, which will be subject to Senate approval once Gaetz is formally nominated by Trump, sets up the potential for a provocative confirmation process. If confirmed, Gaetz would take over the nation’s top law enforcement agency – the same one that pursued a yearslong sex-crimes investigation into the congressman. The Justice Department ultimately decided last year not to pursue criminal charges against him."

The Associated Press reported that "House Speaker Mike Johnson said that Gaetz resigned from Congress on Wednesday, meaning that the House Ethics Committee’s ongoing probe into allegations against Gaetz of child sex trafficking ends, and no report will be issued."  Needless to say, the allegations were serious.
