For several weeks, there has been talk that White House Senior Advisor (and President Trump's son-in-law) Jared Kushner was hard at work on an immigration reform proposal.  No proposal has surfaced yet but the talk continues.

Priscilla Alvarez for CNN reports that, although Kushner's immigration proposal has yet to be completed or released, it is already receiving "an icy reception" from some restrictionist immigration groups.

Kushner said last week that he will present a plan to President Trump shortly.

Roy Beck, president of NumbersUSA, a group that advocates for reduced immigration, has expressed concern with the proposal.  Last week, NumbersUSA released an ad against keeping immigration levels the same, which is what Kushner's proposal is expected to do, arguing that it hurts American wages.

 

Last week, Kushner said he was putting together "a really detailed proposal" to reform the US immigration system, but did not reveal any of the components of his proposal.

A senior administration official told CNN recently that Kushner will focus on border security and legal immigration.   Here are sketches of the rumored proposals.

The border security section would include constructing physical barriers where needed and modernizing ports of entry in the north, south and on the coasts. "This proposal, the official said, would protect rights and respect due process while also preserving the right to detain, adjudicate and remove any individual if need be, especially people trying to circumvent the legal system." 

The second part of the proposal focuses on legal immigration. This proposal would for now keep the level of immigration at the same level it is currently, and work toward a merit-based system based on the Canadian, Australian and New Zealand systems. Trump has previously touted those systems as examples of the direction in which the US should move.
