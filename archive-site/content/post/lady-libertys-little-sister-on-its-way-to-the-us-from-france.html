A replica of the Statue of Liberty that stands on Liberty Island in New York is on its way to the United States. 

The voyage of the new Statue of Liberty to the U.S. is happening at a time when this country's history of slavery and colonialism are being more deeply questioned, and some statues that represent these histories are being taken down.

Sculptor Frédéric-Auguste Bartholdi's original Statue of Liberty, a gift to the U.S. from France, was dedicated on October 28, 1886. The shackles at her feet represent the end of slavery in the United States, but as the New York Times reports, the statue arrived at a time "of Jim Crow racial segregation laws." With the later addition of Emma Lazarus's famous poem and the opening of the inspection station at Ellis Island, the statue has come to represent the welcoming of "your tired, your poor, your huddled masses" to the United States. Yet, the Chinese Exclusion Act was in force at the time the statute was built, and even today our asylum system remains seriously threatened.

The smaller replica statute, which Reuters reports, is 2.8 meters tall, will first stand on Ellis Island over the July 4 holiday, and later be moved to Washington, D.C., for a period of ten years.

If you want to visit the original statue this summer, it will be (partially) reopening in July.
