CrImmigration reports that the Supreme Court today granted cert in a case (Kawashima v. Holder) in which the Ninth Circuit held that filing, and aiding and abetting in filing, a false statement on a corporate tax return in violation of 26 U.S.C. §§ 7206 were aggravated felonies, see INA § 101(a)(43)(M)(i).  For the petition and other materials in the case, see SCOTUSblog.

Judge Diarmuid F. O’Scannlain, joined by Judges Edward Leavy and Consuelo M. Callahan, wrote the Ninth Circuit opinion.  A dissent to the order denying rehearing en banc was filed by Judge Susan Graber, joined by Judges Kim Wardlaw and Richard Paez.

The cert petition identified a conflict between the Ninth Circuit and the Third Circuit.   Note that the Ninth Circuit panel, which included a trio of relatively conservative judges, ruled against the immigrant in the case.  It thus cannot be said that this is a case in which the "liberal" Ninth Circuit once again has overstepped its bounds on the issue of immigration.

By the way, we still are awaiting a decision on the Arizona employer sanction/business licensing law, which might tell us something about the Court's current thinking on federal premption of state and local efforts to regulate immigration and immigrants.
