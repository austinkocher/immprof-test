For immigrants to the United States, the economic cost of becoming a citizen is crushingly high and is about to get higher. For example, according to the National Immigration Forum, a proposed U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) rule could take effect soon that would increase the naturalization application fee 83 percent—from $640 to $1,170—and would eliminate fee waivers.

 

 The rising fees will make it even harder for immigrants to hire an immigration attorney. Immigrants hire attorneys for a variety of needs including securing or renewing immigration authorization, petitioning family members to come to the United States, fighting removal, and in some cases paying for an immigration detention bond. High costs force some to turn to predatory lenders, who can charge high interest rates; others are simply deported for lack of funds.

 

The Capital Good Fund is helping reduce the financial burden on immigrants living in Florida. The nonprofit, U.S. Treasury-certified Community Development Financial Institution offers equitable loans of $700-$20,000 to cover fees including hiring an immigration attorney. The Fund also helps immigrants in Illinois, Rhode Island and Delaware and soon will be expanding loan services to Massachusetts.

 

The Capital Good Fund is reaching out to immigrants to help make their American Dream a reality.
