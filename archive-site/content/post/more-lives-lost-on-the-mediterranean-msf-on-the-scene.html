On Wednesday, a fishing boat carrying some 600 migrants capsized on the Mediterranean. A distress call led an Irish navy vessel to approach the fishing boat packed with migrants. On seeing their rescuers, migrants apparently moved to one side of the fishing boat, causing it to capsize.

The UNHCR reports that 400 people were rescued from the sea. As many as 200 may have drowned.

Medecins Sans Frontieres (MSF), better known in the U.S. as Doctors Without Borders, was one of the first groups to arrive at the scene.


MSF's Dignity I

MSF operates three boats in the Mediterranean (the Phoenix, The Bourbon Argos, and the Dignity I), each manned by experienced search and rescue crews. The group posted dramatic video footage from Wednesday's rescue to twitter.

MSF's project coordinator on the scene, Juan Matías, reports: “It was a horrific sight, people desperately clinging to lifebelts, boats and anything they could to fighting for their lives, amidst people drowning, and those who had already died.”
