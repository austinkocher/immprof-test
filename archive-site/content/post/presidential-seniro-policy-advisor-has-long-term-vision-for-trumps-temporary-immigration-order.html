President Trump earlier this week issued a proclamation suspending immigration to the United States for sixty days.  Nick Miroff and Josh Dawsey for the Washington Post that presidential senior policy adviser Stephen Miller told White House supporters in a private call that the president’s proclaimation will usher in broader long-term changes to American society that he has long advocated, even though the 60-day suspension has been publicly characterized as a “pause.”

Miller, the chief architect of the president’s immigration agenda, spoke to a group of Trump supporters in an off-the-record call about the proclamation. "Although the White House had seen the move as something that would resonate with Trump’s political base, the administration instead was facing criticism from immigration hard-liners who were disappointed that the order does not apply to temporary foreign workers despite Trump pitching it as helping to protect jobs for Americans."

According to Miroff and Dawsey, Miller told the group that subsequent measures were under consideration that would restrict guest worker programs, but the “the most important thing is to turn off the faucet of new immigrant labor,” he said, according to a recording obtained by The Washington Post. Miller indicated that the strategy is part of a long-term vision and not seen only as a stopgap.
