About a decade ago, Hazleton, Pennsylvania made the national headlines with a tough as nails immigration enforcement law focused on new Latino immigrants.   In a series of decisions, with the Supreme Court ultimately denying review, the federal courts held that the Hazleton law was preempted by federal immigration law.  Just last week, a federal court ordered Hazleton to pay $1.4 million in attorneys' fees to the plaintiffs in that successful lawsuit.

NPR provides an update on how, despite the strong resistance to the new Latino population in town, Latino migrants have revitalized Hazleton.  It was just another struggling coal city until a wave of Latino immigrants came to town in 2006. Then-Mayor (now member of Congress) Lou Barletta tried to close the door. "We want people to know that Hazleton is probably the strictest city in the United States for illegal aliens," he said at the time.

Hazleton was a difficult place to be Latino. But the job market was growing, thanks to industrial parks and meatpacking plants. The rent was affordable, and the schools were good. A community emerged and today, Hazleton is 40 percent Latino, among the highest in Pennsylvania.

The city is younger and bigger than it's been in decades. And vacant storefronts downtown have been filled by new businesses. It's a main street transformed. Reggaeton music pours from bodegas. Bustling Mexican restaurants compete with pizzerias. And Spanish mixes fluidly with English to create a Hazleton Spanglish.

"The Latino community right now is the driving force behind a lot of the new businesses that are being opened up," says Neal DeAngelo, with the Downtown Hazleton Alliance for Progress.

Times have changed in Hazleton, Pennsylvania.
