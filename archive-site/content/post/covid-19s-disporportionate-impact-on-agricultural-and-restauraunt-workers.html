 A recent study from the University of California, San Francisco reveals that Latino agricultural and restaurant workers in California have a 60% greater chance of death due to COVID. Latino agricultural and restaurant workers also are at greater risk of being victims of labor trafficking as Polaris documented in a study. 

During the COVID-19 pandemic, agricultural and restaurant workers have been classified as "essential workers” and have continued to work through the crisis with few support measures put in place. The U.S.government has made it easier for employers in the agricultural industry to bring workers with H-2A visas from other countries, namely Mexico. The UCSF study shows that those changes to the H-2A rules have put these workers at greater risk of labor trafficking and mortality during COVID-19.
