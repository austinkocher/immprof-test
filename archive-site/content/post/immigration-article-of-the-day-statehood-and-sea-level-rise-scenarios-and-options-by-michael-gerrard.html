Statehood and Sea-Level Rise: Scenarios and Options by Michael Gerrard, 17 Charleston L. Rev. 579 (2023)

Abstract

In the coming decades, some of the small island states may become submerged or at least uninhabitable as a result of sea level rise. This article discusses what would happen to their status as states and as members of the United Nations, and the citizenship of their population, should that occur. A place is commonly thought to have to meet four requirements before becoming a state: permanent population; defined territory, government; and capacity to enter into relations with other states. International law is unclear as to whether statehood is lost if any of these requirements is lost. This article also discusses the possible alternatives of the territory becomes uninhabitable: moving the population; moving the state; association with another state; government in exile; and deterritorialized state. Finally, it discusses the procedures under which these issues might be resolved.
