More than 4 million Ukraineans have fled their homeland in the last 5 weeks, exceeding the United Nations worst case scenario. ImmigrationProf Blog has covered the US response to Ukranian refugees and more localized resettlement in places like California. European countries that sit closer to the Russian invasion of Ukraine are facing similar choices about their humanitarian obligations to those fleeing the war.

What they've done: As the New York Times editorial board writes, the Council of the European Union passed a directive that grants temporary protected status to Ukrainian nationals and certain legal permanent residents of Ukraine for up to one year. Most Ukrainians already had the right to travel without visas to European Union countries for 90 days. The new measure gives them the right to live, work and attend school in E.U. countries without having to go through the official process of seeking asylum.

What more Europe could do: Ad hoc assistance has been offered to encourage families to take in refugees, including Britain’s “Homes for Ukraine” program that pays families and organizations to take in refugees and has helped 2,7000 Ukranians or Finland's universities offering enrollment to 2000  Ukrainians.. More assistance could be offered to the places where refugees are clustered, and to disperse refugees into additional countries to avoid overcrowding. The European Union has established a platform to match offers of help with those in need. Seven countries, including Norway, Liechtenstein and Switzerland, have pledged to take in some 15,000 of the Ukrainians now in Moldova.
