Will the E-Verify Program Check Illegal Immigrants? The USA is a nation with as many as 11 million illegal immigrants and counting, and as much as immigration reforms try to tackle this problem, it seems to be an insurmountable task. Among the main grouses against illegal immigrants are the fact that they take the jobs that should by all rights go to locals, they boost unemployment, they raise taxes because they take advantage of public services like education and other welfare programs without paying their share, and they’re often perceived as instigating and being involved in criminal activities. And if you want to go a step further, according to freelance writer Donald Collins, immigrants are contributing to pollution and negating the “eco-friendly” measures being emphasized by the administration across the country by overpopulating the cities they inhabit.

Identifying illegal immigrants would be much easier if E-Verify, the Internet-based program run by the Department of Homeland Security in tandem with Social Security Administration which compares information from an employee’s Employee Eligibility Verification Form I-9 to data from a U.S government records, was made mandatory instead optional as it is now. Perhaps this move could also stop the raids on workplaces that seek to weed out undocumented workers, but which usually leads to the arrest of people for minor offenses.

However, there are many people who are against this move – they claim that it could destabilize the agricultural economy which is heavily dependent on undocumented immigrants, and this in turn could spell the death knell for the millions of other jobs held by American citizens that are directly and indirectly related to agriculture. It could mean that America would have to import food and export jobs. Also, they are of the opinion that illegal immigrants are willing to do jobs that Americans aren’t, but this could actually mean that they’re willing to work for dirt cheap wages and accept working conditions that are not exactly ideal.

Nothing certainly justifies using undocumented workers for any kind of job, and employers who encourage this practice must be fined heavily, but will the enforcement of the E-Verify program help bring down the number of illegal immigrants? Employers must cooperate for this program to bear fruit, and even if they do, the thriving market in fake Social Security numbers makes it hard to spot illegal immigrants and makes this exercise pointless.

However, the U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services Agency hopes to use passports and documents that contain photographic identification to weed out the genuine from the fake. Only time will tell if E-Verify is made mandatory and if it helps in keeping the number of illegal immigrants down.
