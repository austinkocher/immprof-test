The Guardian's coverage of the health care woes of a migrant named Ronaldo will churn your stomach.

Ronaldo fled Guatemala after being shot in the head. (His life before the shooting was no cakewalk -- he was orphaned at a tender age following the murder of his pro-indigenous-rights father.) 

Ronaldo fled to the United States following his shooting. He survived metering in Mexico, further violence in that country while waiting his turn to enter the U.S., and then, finally, landed in U.S. immigration detention.

In detention, Ronaldo "would bleed out of his eyes, ears and nose. Other days, he’d lie on the floor, dizzy or barely conscious." He spent time in solitary as a form of medical isolation. He was treated with ibuprofen.

Ronaldo's story presents a harrowing look at the state of medical care in immigration detention facilities.
