Fatou Bensouda, second in command in the Office of the Prosecutor at The Hague-based International Criminal Court, will speak on "Gender Violence and International Criminal Law"  from 5-7 p.m. on Monday, March 8, at UC Davis School of Law. Her address, sponsored by the law school's California International Law Center at King Hall (CILC), corresponds with International Women's Day.

As Deputy Prosecutor at the ICC, Bensouda pursues investigations of persons accused of genocide, crimes against humanity, and war crimes, ranging from sexual violence to forced recruitment of child soldiers, in many of the world's conflict-ridden places, in Darfur, Congo, Central African Republic, Guinea, Kenya, Uganda. Interviews with her about this work were featured in the film "The Reckoning," a 2009 documentary about the ICC which aired on PBS' "Frontline."

Before joining the ICC in 2004, Bensouda served as Attorney General, the top law enforcement officer, in the West African country where she was born, The Gambia. Bensouda played key roles in negotiations at the United Nations and at the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS). Her long advocacy for the rights of women led human rights groups to applaud her ICC appointment. This is Bensouda's first trip to California. Her visit is supported by the Planethood Foundation, established by Donald Ferencz, son of former Nuremberg prosecutor Benjamin Ferencz, to support programs that focus on international criminal justice. Cosponsors include the International Law Society, the Journal of International Law and Policy, the Criminal Law Association, and the Humanitarian Aid Legal Organization, and the UC Davis Women's Resource and Research Center.

Introducing Bensouda will be the Honorable Ramona J. Garrett, Judge of the California Superior Court, Solano County.
