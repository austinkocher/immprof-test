The race for District Attorney (DA) in Los Angeles is heating up and promises to be one of the most important elections in the country this fall. The winner will become the head of the largest local prosecutor office in the country. Los Angeles is also the county that is home to the largest immigrant population in the nation.

The race between current Los Angeles County DA Jackie Lacey and George Gascón, a former LAPD officer who most recently served as DA in San Francisco, is raising some of the crucial questions facing the U.S. criminal legal system. Opposition to the incumbent Lacey have increasingly focused on her failure to prosecute police officers who engage in unlawful use of force. For some time now, weekly protests have been taking place every Wednesday outside City Hall calling for her resignation.

The Los Angeles Times has endorsed Gascón, and this weekend the Mayor of Los Angeles Eric Garcetti also announced that he would support Gascón.

If elected, Mr. Gascón promises to institute many reforms in how the DA’s office operates. With respect to immigration, he told The Appeal that he would “probably look at creating an immigration unit within the office that would have immigration lawyers to help other lawyers in the office make sure that our work is consistent with best practices around immigration consequences.” In this way, he would join other progressive prosecutors around the country who have begun to take immigrant rights into account in charging crimes and plea bargaining. Mr. Gascón himself is an immigrant, having come to the United States from Cuba in the 1960s with his family.
