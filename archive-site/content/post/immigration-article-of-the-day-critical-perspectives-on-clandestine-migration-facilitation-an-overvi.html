Critical Perspectives on Clandestine Migration Facilitation: An Overview of Migrant Smuggling Research by Gabriella Sanchez, University of Texas at El Paso

Media viewers are bombarded with images of migrants and asylum seekers riding atop trains or being rescued after being stranded in the ocean or the desert for days. Around the world, many of these tragedies are attributed to the actions of migrant smugglers, who are usually depicted as men organized in criminal networks who prey on migrants and asylum seekers. Many smugglers do take advantage of the naiveté and helplessness of migrants, refugees, and asylum seekers. However, the relationships that emerge between smugglers and those who rely on their services are much more complex, and quite often, significantly less heinous than what media and law enforcement suggest.

This paper provides an overview of contemporary, empirical scholarship on clandestine migration facilitation. It argues clandestine migration is not merely the domain of criminal groups. Rather, it also involves protection mechanisms crafted within migrant and refugee communities. Yet amid concerns over national and border security, and the reemergence of nationalism, said strategies have become increasingly stigmatized and perceived as an inherently criminal activity. This paper constitutes an attempt to rethink the framework in everyday narratives of irregular migration facilitation.  Download the PDF of the article
