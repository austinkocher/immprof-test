Today DHS and DOL announced an interim final rule proposing comprehensive changes to the H-1B visa program. Among other changes, the rule narrows the definition of “specialty occupations” that traditionally has included computer engineers, software developers, lawyers, accountants, and physicians, among a variety of professionals. It also raises the wages that U.S. companies must pay foreign workers, making them less attractive to employers than American workers. 

Administration officials framed the changes as a way to protect American jobs during the coronavirus pandemic, though the Trump administration has long expressed a desire to overhaul the program as part of its efforts to reduce the number of foreign workers in the United States.

Opponents of the rule include immigration advocates and tech companies that rely on the H-1B program to hire top talent and have long supported or even tried to extend it. Immigration lawyers predict that the rule will be challenged in court.

The DOL rule is effective immediately, even before considering public comments, and the DHS rule will take effect in 60 days. The DHS rule “Strengthening the H-1B Nonimmigrant Visa Classification Program” is available here. The rule follows another ban on nonimmigrant worker visas that was enjoined in federal court last week.
