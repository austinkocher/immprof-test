Nevada's Struggle With Immigration May Have a Savior by Arkady Bukh, Esq.  

In recent correspondence with Representative Jason Chaffetz, chairman of the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform, Nevada's Jason Frierson urged the chairman to approve immigration reform to help Nevada's economy and help families stay together.

"We can protect our borders as we give these Nevada families the path to citizenship they deserve," Frierson wrote.

Frierson knows what he's talking about when it comes to the intersection of immigration and Nevada's economic struggles.

According to the Census Bureau's recently released 2016 population estimates, the impact of illegal immigrant inflow was the greatest in the Southwest — Arizona, California, Nevada, and Texas.  Florida, New York, and Illinois were not far behind.

Undocumented immigrants streaming into Nevada for work are finding two prongs of a legal fork. On one hand is law enforcement led by Homeland Security and the Border Patrol who are charged with monitoring, detaining and deporting illegal immigrants. On the other are the progressive laws which allow for illegal immigrants, who meet strict criteria, to post immigration bond and bail out of jail while they await their immigration hearing.

The construction industry in Nevada took a hit in the 2007-2009 recession and is still inching back to pre-recession numbers. The state could use some help, and it may have a David ready to do battle with Goliath.

Ruben Kihuen

Kihuen feels humbly honored to be in the American House of Representatives. Politics though was not his priority growing up.

"My first dream was to play pro soccer," Kihuen recently told Las Vegas' Review-Journal.

Today, following his work as an aide to Senate Democratic Leader Harry Reid and some time in the Nevada Senate, Kihuen wants to utilize his Congressional seat to change the nation's immigration laws.

"The most vital thing a representative can do is provide a voice to his constituents," Kihuen said.

Kihuen, a Democrat, represents Nevada's 4th Congressional District which covers Las Vegas and middle portions of the state. About 30-percent of the district's population is Latino with about 7-percent Asian according to recent Census Bureau figures.

Congress has often struggled with reforming immigration. Both sides of the aisle agree that the current archaic system needs streamlining, but they parties disagree on the approach.

Immigration was pushed into the recent campaign when then-presidential candidate called for a border wall and promised to ramp up deportation of 11 million persons in America illegally.

Kihuen, born in Mexico, came to America with his parents when he was 8. At the time he was living in America on an expired visa but has not become a naturalized citizen.

"At one time, I was undocumented," Kihuen said. "It's useful to have that viewpoint."

Kihuen was recently assigned to the House Financial Services Committee which has oversight of the nation's housing laws, an important issue for Nevada which was crushed during the Great Recession.

Kihuen plans to hold the new administration accountable. The congressman said he was offended during the campaign when immigrants were categorized as rapists and thieves.

"People are tired of the rhetoric. I am as well," he said.

Kihuen supports reform that would open a path to citizenship for undocumented immigrants presently working and living in the nation.

Kihuen believes that is what makes America great. He came from a family given that opportunity.

"I am indebted to this nation," Kihuen told the newspaper.
