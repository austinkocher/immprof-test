--- title: "Businesses, Economy Depend on Immigrant Contributions" date: 2016-11-02T04:19:38 url: "/post/businesses-economy-depend-on-immigrant-contributions/" aliases: ["/immigration/2016/11/businesses-economy-depend-on-immigrant-contributions.html"] type: post year: "2016" category: "Current Affairs" category_slug: "current-affairs" byline: "Immigration Prof" ---

Studies of startup activity and major companies alike indicate that immigrants comprise more than 27 percent of those who start new businesses, as well as more than 40 percent of those who started Fortune 500 companies.

Case study: Chobani. A New York Times article describes the growth of the company, which was founded by a Turkish immigrant and now employs more than 2,000 workers, including hundreds of refugees.

“He’s the xenophobe’s nightmare,” Kenneth Roth, executive director of Human Rights Watch, said of the founder, Hamdi Ulukaya. “Here’s an immigrant who isn’t competing for jobs, but is creating jobs big time. It runs completely counter to the far-right narrative.”   Incidentally, Ulukaya has been attacked for hiring refugees and encouraging other employers to do the same.

In addition, according to a recent study from the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, the children of immigrants contribute more in taxes as adults than the native-born population.

“Quite simply, we can’t afford to perpetuate a system that puts us at a competitive disadvantage,” Jimmy Kemp, president of the Jack Kemp Foundation, writes today in a Hill op-ed co-authored by Ali Noorani, executive director of the National Immigration Forum. “American workers will benefit from a new immigration process that allows everyone to contribute to our nation and help us reach our full potential in the global economy.”
