In "Biden Plans To Undo Trump-Era Curbs On Immigration Judges," Alyssa Aquino for Law360 reports that the Biden administration moved to codify into federal regulation immigration judges' authority to pause low-priority removal cases as part of a proposal that looks to undo a Trump-era regulation that limited immigration judge powers.  

"In the Trump administration's last weeks, it finalized sweeping changes to the immigration courts and the Board of Immigration Appeals aimed at speeding up the deportation process, including by restricting jurists' ability to dismiss or reopen removal cases, setting strict filing deadlines for all cases and eliminating the board's authority to hear cases by discretion.

The U.S. Department of Justice proposed knocking back those changes [yesterday], but floated the additional step of enshrining immigration judges' discretionary authority to administratively close, or pause, deportation cases."
