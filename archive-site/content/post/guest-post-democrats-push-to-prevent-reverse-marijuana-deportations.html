Marijuana by taki Lau

Guest post by Jacob Downs, 3L at the University of Oklahoma College of Law

With the recent push across the country to legalize marijuana and expunge prior marijuana-related crimes, House Assistant Speaker Ben Ray Luján (D-NM) filed a bill to amend marijuana deportability and inadmissibility grounds. Senator Cory Booker (D-NJ) originally introduced the Removing Marijuana from Deportable Offenses in June. The act changes the inadmissibility grounds and prohibits any offense involving the use, possession, or distribution of marijuana from being grounds for inadmissibility. For deportability, the focus is on INA 237(a)(2)(B)(i) and changes the language of the current marijuana exception from “other than a single offense involving possession for one’s own use of 30 grams or less of marijuana” to “other than offenses involving the use, possession, or distribution of marijuana.” Another important point is that the amendment doesn’t require that the marijuana conviction be from the United States. This act would be a major shift in federal marijuana law. Realistically, this will not pass. But it does signal potential priorities for the Democrats if they were to gain power in the 2020 election.

Also, importantly, it requires any alien deported because of a marijuana conviction to be readmitted and reissued their previous visa, so long as they are otherwise admissible. As we read this week, controlled substances convictions are a major deportability ground and this act would likely reduce those numbers significantly. According to the Human Rights Watch, 34,000 immigrants were deported between 2007 and 2012 for marijuana offenses. This readmission provision, though likely politically unpopular, also signals that the Democrats may pursue other immigration reforms to reverse the Trump administration’s focus on deportation.
