Nicole Rosenthal for Law360 reports on the Board of Immigration Appeals May 5, 2022 ruling in Matter of  German Santos.  The BIA denied a lawful permanent resident's appeal of a removal order, ruling that his conviction under Pennsylvania law for possession of marijuana, a federally controlled substance, is grounds for removal.  The three-member Board panel rejected the appeal of Jose Miguel German Santos, a lawful permanent resident from the Dominican Republic.

Deputy Chief Appellate Immigration Judge Garry D. Malphrus, and Appellate Immigration Judges Keith E. Hunsucker and Aaron R. Petty sat on the panel for the Board of Immigration Appeals.

"Because the respondent was convicted of an offense relating to a substance that is controlled under Federal law, he committed an offense categorically relating to a controlled substance violation. Thus, the respondent is removable," the BIA said.

German Santos had been convicted of possession with intent to deliver a controlled substance and driving under the influence after a 2017 traffic incident. Police also recovered 138 grams of marijuana from German Santos' car.

The BIA ruling is a reminder that, even with the growing de-criminilaztion of marijuana, marijuana convictions can result in removal.
