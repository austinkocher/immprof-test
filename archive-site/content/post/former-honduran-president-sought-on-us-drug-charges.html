The Los Angeles Times reports on the arrest of the former President of Honduras on drug trafficking charges.  He had been one of the U.S.'s top allies in Central America.

U.S. prosecutors identified President Juan Orlando Hernández as a co-conspirator in a drug smuggling case against his brother.  But the Biden administration was reluctant to go after the sitting president.  He stepped down in January.

"Eric Olson, an expert on Central America, said Biden’s approach then was to glad-hand and amiably try to persuade the presidents to cooperate with the U.S. goal of reducing migration.

`At the time, the U.S. policy was to cajole,' said Olson, a fellow at the nonpartisan Wilson Center think tank in Washington. `Eventually, they came to the conclusion that these guys were not cajole-able. The scenario has changed, they finally woke up and smelled the coffee and realized how deeply corrupt' the officials were."

At a 2016 summit aimed at deepening ties between the U.S. and Central America, then-Vice President Joe Biden was photographed with the Honduran President.  At that time, `record numbers of young migrant children were fleeing violence and poverty in the region and arriving at the U.S. border. Biden, who was tasked with addressing the crisis by President Obama, lobbied Congress for more than a billion dollars in aid for Honduras, Guatemala and El Salvador to address the `root causes' of migration, more than tripling existing U.S. aid to the region."
