The Arizona Republic reports that Sheriff Joe Arpaio (Maricopa County Sheriff's Office (Arizona)) will face a civil contempt hearing in federal court this spring. U.S. District Judge Murray Snow ruled after a Thursday conference that he will initiate a civil contempt of court proceedings against Arpaio during a four-day mini trial in April.  Snow has repeatedly threatened to level criminal or civil contempt proceedings against the office. 

In a written statement filed earlier this month, Arpaio's attorneys apologized for general missteps while maintaining that any gaffes were unintentional, placing the brunt of the blame onto former employees. "Sheriff Arpaio readily concedes that mistakes have been made in the communication and, in some instances, implementation of Court Orders," the statement reads. "He genuinely regrets those mistakes, and is committed to working with the Court Monitor to carry out existing directives and minimize further mistakes."

The contempt hearing arises from a class-action civil-rights case that alleged the Maricopa County Sheriff's Office's immigration sweeps amounted to discriminatory policing against Latinos. Judge Snow agreed and issued an injunction but has become increasingly agitated over perceived resistance by Sheriff's Office and outright defiance from Arpaio.

At a hearing last month, Snow identofied three issues of "grave concern"over compliance with court orders:

— The office's failure to disclose information prior to trial, including audio and video recordings and other evidence, that may have broadened the scope of the court's actions;

— Arpaio's public statements that he would continue to arrest undocumented immigrants and turn them over to federal immigration authorities;

— Commanders' failure to relay the court's directives to rank-and-file deputies.
