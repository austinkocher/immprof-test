Former Maricopa County (Arizona) Sheriff Joe Arpaio, now running for the U.S. Senate, is in the news again.  The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit (Ronald M. Gould, Richard C. Tallman, and Paul J. Watford, Circuit Judges), in an opinion by Judge Watford, ruled, that Maricopa County could be held liable for Sheriff Arpaio's law enforcement policies.

As discussed in this Arizona Republic article, the court rejected the argument that Maricopa County was not liable for the racial-profiling traffic-stop policies of former Sheriff Arpaio.  Arpaio himself was found first in civil contempt of court and then criminal contempt for continuing to carry out the discriminatory practices despite a court order. He was pardoned last year by President Donald Trump on the criminal contempt charges.

By The Arizona Republic's last tally in September, the cost to the county of the litigation was approaching $70 million, with an additional $26 million expected to be spent in 2018 on legal fees, new technology, training and a monitor to oversee the compliance efforts.

Arpaio and his office had been sued by the American Civil Liberties Union and the U.S. Department of Justice over his immigration-tinged policies.  In addition, the U.S. Department of Justice alleged that the Maricopa County the Sheriff's Office engaged in discriminatory police practices and that the agency retaliated against the sheriff's critics.
