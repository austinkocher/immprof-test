The Trump administration has promised to expand immigrant detention and to continue to use private contractors in that detention.  As I wrote for The Conversation, immigrant detention often is challenged in the courts.  The Los Angeles Times reports on a lawsuit brought by former detainees against the operator of an immigrant detention facility, GEO Group, alleging the company was unfairly enriched by a program of forced labor by immigrants. A federal judge last week ruled that the plaintiffs could move forward with a class-action lawsuit against GEO Group, which operates dozens of private prisons and detention centers across the country.  The company contracts with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement to operate a 1,500-bed center in Aurora, says it has done nothing wrong, noting in a court filing that such programs are “fully authorized by the federal government — and have been for decades.” 

One of the class representatives in the case, Grisel Xahuentitla, while in a GEO immigrant detention facility in Colorado, claims to have spent six hours a day passing out trays before meals, scrubbing toilets and scouring showers.  She allegedly was participating in a voluntary work program that allows detainees to earn $1 a day to help with the upkeep of the facility.
