Dr. Marty Cetron, the U.S. government's top public health expert at the CDC Division of Global Migration and Quarantine, told Congress that he refused to approve a policy allowing mass expulsions at the U.S.-Mexico border because he believed the measure was unfounded based on public health evidence. Furthermore, he believed the measure unfairly stigmatized migrants as spreaders of COVID-19: 

An epidemic of disease can be followed by an epidemic — an inappropriate epidemic of stigma and misrepresentation of where the problem is.

During an interview with the House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Crisis, Dr. Marty Cetron called the policy, known as Title 42, a "wholesale border closure" that suspended the rights of migrants and "risked the misuse of a public health authority," according to a transcript of the interview. (The interview had not previously been publicly disclosed, although it was originally conducted in May, when the CDC determined that it would terminate its public health order.)

In 2020, Dr. Cetron refused to sign the order to invoke Title 42 out of suspicion that there were other motives for the public health order. CDC Director Robert Redfield signed it in March 2020. Several other high level officials resigned, including State Department official and former Yale law school dean Harold Koh who said the policy was "illegal" and "inhumane."

Upon the CDC declaration that it would lift the order, the Biden administration's DHS Secretary Mayorkas continued to say Title 42 was a health policy, not a migration policy chosen by his federal agency: "Title 42 is not an immigration authority, but rather a public health authority used by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention to protect against the spread of communicable disease. Title 42 remains in place until May 23 and, until then, DHS will continue to expel single adults and families encountered at the Southwest border."

The policy enacted during the Trump administration remained in force during the first year of the Biden administration; efforts to lift it this year have stalled in litigation and Congressional stalemate.

Dr. Cetron believed then and advocates believe now there are alternative measures to Title 42 for containing COVID-19 without shutting down the asylum system, such as using testing and vaccination.
