Amy Taxin for the Associated Press reports that, in the time of COVID, immigrant detention beds that the U.S. government pays for under guaranteed contracts with private parties are going unused as detention numbers have been low.  For example, "[a] sprawling, privately run detention center in the wind-swept California desert town of Adelanto could house nearly 2,000 migrants facing the prospect of deportation. These days, though, it’s nearly empty. . . . The Adelanto facility is an extreme example of how the U.S. government’s use of guaranteed minimum payments in contracts with private companies to house immigrant detainees might have a potential financial downside. In these contracts, the government commits to pay for a certain number of beds, whether they’re used or not."
