Despite the many claims that the lifting of the Title 42 order would result in catastrophe or worse, border crossings are down.  Ashley Lopez for NPR reports on the lack of a crisis on the U.S./Mexico border after the end of the Title 42 border closure.  Border residents describe a very different border than one in crisis. 

When the Title 42 order was lifted last week, there were expectations that it would lead to an increase in the number of migrants coming into the U.S..

In the past several days, however, there has been an overall decrease in border crossings.

Rudy Flores works in downtown Brownsville, Texas on the U.S./Mexico border. He says a lot of what he hears in the media often does not match his experience.

"They are making it seem worse than it is," Flores said. "It's just calm. They are just trying to get somewhere."

An increase in attention from media and policymakers during national debates over immigration policy generally generates some resentment among people who actually live along the border. Flores says this time is no different.
