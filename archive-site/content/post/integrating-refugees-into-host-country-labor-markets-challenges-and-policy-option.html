As the world copes with record numbers of refugees, social and integration services are sometimes overlooked as emergency response takes precedence. Yet without sufficient support to integrate newcomers into their host society and get them into jobs commensurate with their skills and experience, the ramifications of the crises could persist for generations.

A new report from the Migration Policy Institute’s Transatlantic Council on Migration, Integrating Refugees into Host Country Labor Markets: Challenges and Policy Options, examines the barriers refugees and asylum seekers face getting into the workforce. Of all immigrant groups, refugees and asylum seekers historically have faced the greatest difficulty locating work at their skill level. The result is lower overall employment compared to labor migrants and natives, as well as high numbers of refugees working in low-skilled sectors, often in jobs for which they are overqualified.

The author, MPI Europe Senior Policy Analyst Maria Vincenza Desiderio, cites a number of factors that explain the barriers to integration encountered by refugees and asylum seekers, including limited control over their final destination, interrupted work experience, trauma, and difficulty accessing good work and training opportunities close to shelters or other housing. She also identifies several policy priorities that support labor market integration, including early skills assessment and training, recognition of foreign academic and professional credentials, and employer engagement.

The report encourages policymakers to start integration initiatives early, thoroughly evaluate integration programs, and clearly communicate integration prospects and expected returns on investments.

The report is the sixth in a Transatlantic Council series focused on promising practices to promote the longer-term social and economic inclusion of refugees. Earlier reports examined the growing use of livelihood programs for refugees, Turkey’s evolving humanitarian protection policies, the Southeast Asian response to the maritime migration crisis in the Bay of Bengal and Andaman Sea, Eritrean refugee movements through the Horn of Africa, and the expansion of legal labor and study mobility channels for refugees.
