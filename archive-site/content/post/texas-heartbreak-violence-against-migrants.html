I don't know about you, but I'm still reeling from the news out of Texas this week.

First came the deadly shootings at the Allen outlet mall. That was an initial gut punch because I've been there. Many, many times. So that story had some immediacy for me: I could picture myself there, with my kids. Then today I read WaPo's write up of one of the victims of the shooting: Aishwarya Thatikonda. I encourage you to click through, but know this: She was an engineer who came to the U.S. from India. She was only 26 but had already been here for about 5 years. The paper includes details about the desperate search for Thatikonda after the shooting--from hospital to hospital to the morgue. And now she'll have to be shipped back home to India. 

Second came the news out of Brownsville, Texas: a driver ran into a group of migrants sitting in front of a shelter. I heard that news a little late--I was shut up in the office at work, completing my final exam. Today, WaPo tells me that the driver is being charged with manslaughter as 8 people died in the crash. I saw the video of the crash. I understand why news outlets aren't showing it. You can't edit out the people who clearly died. That's a lot. But it really looked like murder to me. A car driving extremely fast just mowed down (literally, like a lawn mower) a row of individuals sitting on the sidewalk. I'm not sure how that's manslaughter.

I don't know. It's early in the morning here. I'm not at my most articulate. I'm just left feeling from the combination of these two stories that the value of migrant life isn't worth a whole lot. We immprofs already knew that in the sense of the country's tolerance for border deaths. We knew that in the sense of the country's tolerance for dangerous working conditions for migrants. This just feels a little different. Not tolerating their presence at the mall or sitting on a sidewalk.

Heartbreaking.

-KitJ
