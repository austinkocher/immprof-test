News from Oklahoma.   A federal judge yesterday enjoined Oklahoma from enforcing its new law that would make it a crime to live in the state without legal immigration status.

A Trump appointee, U.S. District Judge Bernard M. Jones issued a preliminary injunction to go into effect while their lawsuit challenging it continues. The law would have taken effect next week.

The lawsuit in federal court challenges the law, which makes it a crime punishable by up to two years in prison, to live in Oklahoma without legal immigration status. Similar laws passed in Texas and Iowa also are subject to challenges from the Justice Department.

The Justice Department says the Oklahoma statute violates the U.S. Constitution and is asking the court to declare it invalid and bar the state from enforcing it.

Judge Jones' order says Oklahoma “may have understandable frustrations with the problems caused by illegal immigration," but that "the State may not pursue policies that undermine federal law.”
