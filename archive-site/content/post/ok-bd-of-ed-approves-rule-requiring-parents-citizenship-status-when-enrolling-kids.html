--- title: "OK Bd of Ed Approves Rule Requiring Parents' Citizenship Status When Enrolling Kids" date: 2025-01-28T15:31:03 url: "/post/ok-bd-of-ed-approves-rule-requiring-parents-citizenship-status-when-enrolling-kids/" aliases: ["/immigration/2025/01/ok-bd-of-ed-approves-rule-requiring-parents-citizenship-status-when-enrolling-kids.html"] type: post year: "2025" category: "Current Affairs" category_slug: "current-affairs" byline: "Immigration Prof" ---

Today, the Oklahoma Board of Education approved the following new rule regarding enrollment of kids in public K-12:

**

(4) In order to assess statewide and local educational needs, including without limitation, student needs, language and cultural barriers, current and future needs for English as a Second Language (“ESL”) teachers, tutors and tutoring programs, current and future transportation needs, programs, and anticipated future funding needs, a parent or a legal guardian of a child, or an emancipated minor, shall provide proof of United States (“U.S. ”) citizenship at the time of enrollment. Proof of U.S. Citizenship or legal immigration status may be shown through one of the following:

  1. Certified U.S. birth certificate;
  2. A valid, unexpired U.S. Passport;
  3. A valid Oklahoma Driver License, originally issued after November 1, 2007;
  4. A valid Oklahoma Identification Card, originally issued after November 1, 2007;
  5. A Naturalization/Citizenship Certificate;
  6. Consular Report of Birth Abroad;
  7. Unexpired Permanent Resident Card;
  8. Unexpired Employment Authorization Card; or
  9. Valid, unexpired foreign passport with a valid U.S. visa and valid I-94 issued by DHS.

(5) Each school district shall record the number of students enrolled within the district for which a parent or a legal guardian of a child, or the emancipated minor could not provide proof of citizenship or legal immigration status as outlined in paragraph (d)(3) due to the lack of citizenship or legal immigration status of the student.The school district shall submit only information of the total number of students that lack of documentation, excluding any personally identifiable information to the Oklahoma State Department of Education (“OSDE”).

(6) Nothing in this Section shall be construed to prohibit, or inhibit, any child from receiving the education they are entitled to receive under the United States Constitution, the Oklahoma Constitution, or state or federal law.

(7) No data outlined in this Section shall be used to record an individual student’s citizenship status or legal immigration status.

**

Here are a few key things to know:

This rule isn't yet active. It has to be approved by the legislature and the governor. But it seems highly likely that they'll approve it given the former is controlled by Republicans and the latter is Republican. But, then again, our Republican AG is before SCOTUS trying to prevent the Oklahoma Statewide Charter School Board from authorizating a religious charter school. So far, he hasn't commented.

The language of the rule is ambiguous. See the highlighting above. Whose citizenship needs to be proven? At their meeting today, the Board of Ed clarified that it's looking to collect information on parents, not students. The rule language itself, however, is just unclear.

If passed, the rule is unlikely to affect large numbers of Oklahomans until Fall 2025. Enrollment typically happens once a year, the summer before a new school school starts. While there are always transfers throughout the year, those don't represent the majority of folks.

Expect a lawsuit and a motion for injunctive relief if this rule passes. The National Immigration Law Center is likely to sue, as it sued Alabama in 2011 when it tried to pass a similar law.

As just noted, about a decade ago, Alabama passed legislation seeking, among other things, to collect data about student citizenship. There were two suits to enjoin the law -- one brought by private actors including the NILC and one brought by the US government. The provision regarding student enrollment was enjoined as presenting unconstitutional "obstacles to the ability of an undocumented child to obtain an education." Hisp. Int. Coal. of Alabama (HICA) v. Governor of Alabama, 691 F.3d 1236 (11th Cir. 2012).

There are differences between the Alabama law and the Oklahoma rule. (1) AL involved a state statute, OK involves a rule from the Bd of Ed. (2) AL sought info about student citizenship, OK seeks info about parent citizenship. (3) The AL statute was explicitly passed to reduce illegal immigration while the OK rule is ostensibly about collecting data--though outside events like the lawsuit against the feds for the cost of educating undocumented kids suggests a similar intent. I don't see any of those resulting in a different outcome where the request for citizenship information alone creates an unlawful obstacle to access to public education.

The stated reasons for this data collection do not appear to justify the burden on kids' rights to basic education. Paragraph (4) above says it's necessary to know about ESL needs. But the enrollment process already asks about the parents' preferred language and whether the enrolled student needs language assistance. In terms of all the other reasons for collecting the data -- transportation, etc. -- those seem driven entirely by enrollment numbers and have nothing to do with the citizenship of parents.

There you have it. Another fun day on the front lines in Oklahoma.
