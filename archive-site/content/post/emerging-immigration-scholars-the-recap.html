the imm-prawfs: Chen, Ryo, Lee, and me

UCLA held what I hope will be its inaugural Emerging Immigration Scholars Conference this past weekend. The conference brought together junior scholars from a variety of disciplines - history, sociology, ethnography, American studies, Ethnic studies, political science and, of course law.

Here's a quick recap of the works-in-progress that were floated by the law profs in attendance:

  • Ming Hsu Chen (Colorado) presented on the role that the federal government should play to successfully integrate immigrants to the United States.
  • Emily Ryo (USC) is writing on her most recent empirical study on the legal attitudes of immigrants. This time, she's investigating the legal attitudes of immigrant detainees!
  • Jennifer Lee (Temple) is evaluating the impact that guest worker programs have on U.S. low-wage workers and how "cultural mythologies" (about both U.S. workers and migrants) help to justify the programs.
  • I'm suggesting that individuals around the world should be allowed to swap their citizenships, and even be allowed to make trades that involve a side payment of cash.

It was wonderful to hear about not only these projects but so many that are happening in other fields where folks are writing on such far-ranging topics as anti-migrant attacks in post-apartheid South Africa (Marcel Paret - Utah) to apple pickers in Virginia (David Trouille - John Marshall).

We hope to report on the projects (across fields) as they are finalized. Stay tuned.
