This Casenote in the Supreme Court issue of the Harvard Law Review looks at the Supreme Court's decision in Department of State v. Munoz.  It concludes:

"Recently, in Department of State v. Muñoz, the Supreme Court held that the right to marriage and family unity does not apply to a U.S. citizen and their noncitizen spouse. In deciding the case based on substantive, rather than procedural, due process, the Court took an opportunity to rewrite the law and undermine existing fundamental rights of marriage. Muñoz is troubling. It shows the current Court doubling down on its commitment to immigration exceptionalism — its practice of failing to apply traditional constitutional principles in the immigration context — and signals how willing this Court is to unravel settled, substantive rights." (footnotes omitted).
