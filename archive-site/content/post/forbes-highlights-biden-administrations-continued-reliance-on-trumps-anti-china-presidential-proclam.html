On June 4, 2020, President Trump issued Presidential Proclamation 10043: Suspension of Entry as Nonimmigrants of Certain Students and Researchers From the People's Republic of China. (Kevin's 2020 post about the proclamation is here.)

Trump's move was bold given that China had been the largest sender of international students studying in the United States. (See, e.g. this Statistica analysis on point as well, this 2017 post, and data from Open Doors.) And international students have long been economically important to colleges and universities across the country, something I've written about.

So today's article from Forbes really jumped out at me. It turns out that consular officials are continuing to enforce Trump's presidential proclamation by denying visas for Chinese graduate students based on the Chinese university they attended. The article highlights the plight of a PhD student from China who completed most of her program in the United States but was unable to renew her visa to make it the final stretch. (They're letting her do it remotely, but still.)

The Biden Administration really has done a lot with regards to immigration through passivity -- just keeping on board Trump era policies and procedures.
