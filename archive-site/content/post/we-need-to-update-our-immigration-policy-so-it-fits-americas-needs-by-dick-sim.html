Back in 2013, immigration reform was being discussed in Congress, much like it has been during this presidential election. At that time, Hillary Clinton stated, based on leaked emails, that being opposed to immigration is “fundamentally un-American” and that "American" is not a national identity but rather is "an intellectual invention."  That same year, Paul Ryan was also talking about America not being a country or a national identity but rather an "intellectual idea." Both of these statements were made in the context of promoting open border immigration.  

There is some truth, however, to both Clinton and Ryan’s attraction to an open borders policy and their statements about America only being an “idea.” But, it’s not quite that simple. America is a complex set of ideas and it only works as well as it does because of a culture and community that supports it. The U.S. has demonstrated extraordinary economic success because of the combination of a system of democratic government and laws combined with a free market capitalistic culture that provides lots of entrepreneurs willing to try and lots of risk capital to support them.  

OPEN BORDERS AND IMMIGRATION LAW  

There are tens of millions of people around the world who would like to emigrate to the United States. With such a large number of people wanting to make it in America, the U.S. is at a crossroads. We can either choose who we want to come or we can let immigrants themselves choose. The difference is between controlled legal immigration and uncontrolled illegal - or open borders - immigration. The people we desire as immigrants - young well-educated professionals - will not come illegally. But we need to invite them. The people who come illegally are typically poor, not afforded a great education and are desperate and willing to take risks to escape the reality of places that offer little promise of improvement. The United States has a choice to make this election year – do we stay with the immigration path we are on or do we need to update our immigration law so that it fits our needs?  

THE ECONOMICS OF IMMIGRATION  

People who believe in open borders argue that all immigrants create economic growth. This is true in that the money that immigrants spend increases overall market demand. The important question is where do these immigrants get their money.  If it is from a job in the private sector and that job did not result from displacing an existing resident then it is a plus to the economy. However, if the new immigrant's source of funds is from the government, then the immigrant’s spending is a destruction of national wealth. Consequently, the number and type of new immigrants should be controlled to match the jobs available. Encouraging more low cost labor to    illegally enter the U.S. just makes it more difficult for the working folks already here legally.    

THE MORAL PURPOSE OF THE USA  

America has industrialized practically all the new technology in the world in the last seventy years. Such technical innovations include electronic hardware, software, new materials, the internet, new disease resistant crops, improved transportation, new drugs, medical diagnostic innovations, robots among others. These technological innovations are the main reason that the world has gotten richer, safer and global poverty has been reduced. In addition to the technical contribution the U.S. has also created many new jobs in the developing world due to its sourcing of components and finished products.  China could not have the progress it has without the demand from the U.S. Viewed this way, America is the engine for progress in the world. America’s responsibility to itself and to the world is to continue to have a high added value economy that continues to be the leader in the world in technical innovation. Right now, Americans are reproducing below their replacement rate so inviting a certain amount of immigrants with certain attributes makes sense.  Open borders immigration will weaken the U.S. economically.
