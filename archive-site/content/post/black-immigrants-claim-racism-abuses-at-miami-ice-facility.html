Latina/o noncitizens are the overwhelming number of those detained and deported.  Black immigrants also have been teh subject of harsh immigration treatment.  The world recently saw photos of Border Patrol officers on horseback chasing Haitian migrants along the U.S./Mexico border.  Now, Nathan Hale for Law360 reports that immigrant advocacy groups have called on U.S. officials to respond to Black immigrants' allegations of a pattern of ongoing anti-Black racism and abuse at an immigration detention center outside of Miami.

UndocuBlack Network, Haitian Bridge Alliance, Freedom for Immigrants, and the National Immigration Project of the National Lawyers Guild demanded an investigation into the allegations and the release of all detainees at Krome North Service Processing Center, which is already the subject of a federal lawsuit by detainees over its response to the COVID-19 pandemic.  The 10-page letter is addressed to officials with the Department of Homeland Security and Immigration and Customs Enforcement, as well as a representative for a private contractor that operates the facility. The letter begins:

"UndocuBlack Network, Haitian Bridge Alliance, Freedom for Immigrants, and the National Immigration Project of the National Lawyers Guild, submit this complaint on behalf of Ernst Francois (A# 041-582-833), Johan Cruyff Jimstar Aceus (A# 060-981-012), and seven other individuals who wish to remain anonymous due to fear of retaliation. All are Black  immigrants, five from Haiti, one from Sierra Leone, one from St. Lucia, one from Jamaica and one from the Bahamas, who are currently detained or have previously been detained at Krome North Service Processing Center and have reported violations of their civil and human rights, including medical neglect, sexual abuse, religious discrimination, retaliation, verbal abuse from guards, anti-Blackness, and egregious COVID-19 endangerment by Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). We urge the DHS Office for Civil Rights and Civil Liberties (CRCL), pursuant to its authority under 6 U.S.C. § 345, to immediately 1) Investigate the medical practices, religious and racial discrimination, sexual abuse, and COVID-19 negligence and 2) recommend the release of all individuals detained at Krome."

"This new complaint unequivocally demonstrates that DHS, much like state and local law enforcement agencies, has an endemic and foundational anti-Blackness problem," Breanne Palmer, interim policy and advocacy director at the UndocuBlack Network, said in a statement. "Whether DHS's violence is highly public at the border, or behind closed doors in detention, we understand how devastating interacting with DHS can be for Black immigrants."
