The Office for Creative Research, We Were Strangers Once Too" in Times Square. Justin Bettman for Times Square Arts.

Last year,Times Square Arts unveiled a project, We Were Strangers Once Too. The team responsible for the sculptural work was the Office for Creative Research (OCR).

From February 7 to March 5, 2017, visitors to midtown Manhattan could walk around the 33 pink and red columns that make up the work, each segment representing a different nationality of New York’s three million immigrants. 

If you face the artwork head on, the poles align, revealing the shape of a heart.

The artwork is the winner of the annual Times Square Valentine Heart Design Competition, determined by Times Square Arts and a number of arts professionals and city officials.

The piece “champions the value of diversity in the city, and specifically the city’s immigrant populations, new and old, at a time when they are increasingly under siege,” according to the project description.

“This is the ninth annual Valentine’s heart,” said Tim Tompkins, president of the Times Square Alliance, at the unveiling. “It’s particularly relevant that this year we are trying to send a message of love, rather than hate, love rather than fear, which seem to be the themes of the day in so many different ways.”

This year, a new winner -- "Window to the Heart" -- is on display.
