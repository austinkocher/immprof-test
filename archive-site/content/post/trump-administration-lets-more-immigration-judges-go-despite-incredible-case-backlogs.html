The immigration courts have a backlog of millions of cases.  Nonetheless, NPR reports that "[a]nother round of immigration judges received an email on Friday informing them they are being let go, NPR has learned, adding to the growing list of immigration court personnel cut by President Trump amid his efforts to speed up deportations of immigrants without legal status."  Fifteen immigration judges reportedly learned that they would be put on leave and that their employment would terminate on July 22, according to the union that represents immigration judges.

On July 3, Massachusetts' two Democratic U.S. senators, Elizabeth Warren and Ed Markey, sent a letter to the EOIR Acting Director raising concerns over a prior round of immigration court layoffs.  "As additional classes reach this mark over the coming months, EOIR must ensure that its conversion decisions are based solely on judges' performance, not their perceived loyalty to the Trump Administration's immigration agenda or any other criteria," Warren and Markey wrote, noting that typically 94% of judges are converted to permanent positions after their probationary period.
