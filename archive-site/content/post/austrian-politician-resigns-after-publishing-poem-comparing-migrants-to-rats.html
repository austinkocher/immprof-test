On Easter Sunday, the Freedom Party of Austria (a far-right nationalist group that is part of the current majority coalition government) published a poem by an Austrian politician named Christian Schilcher who happened to be the deputy mayor of Adolf Hitler's hometown. The title of the poem is "... die Stadtratte (Nagetier mit Kanalisationshintergrund)" which Google translates rather awkwardly to "...the city rat (rodent with sewerage background)."

If you are fluent in German, you can read the poem here.

If, like me, your German skills are nonexistent, you'll have to rely on the news outlets for their take on this poem.

According to the Guardian, "The title is a play on words referring to a categorisation, 'person with migrant background', commonly used in German-language media and government correspondence for ethnic minority or mixed race citizens." As for the content of the poem, the BBC reports that the poem compares migrants to rats who must "share with us the way of life, or quickly hurry away." CNN reports that another line of the poem reads: "if you mix two cultures ... it's as if you destroy them."

The Chancellor of Austria, Sebastian Kurz, called the poem "abominable" as well as "disgusting, inhuman and deeply racist."

While Schilcher initially defended himself against the outpouring of criticism over his poem, he has since resigned.
