Kelly Knaub on Law360 reports on an important case before the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights.  Here is the story: 

The Inter-American Commission on Human Rights has opened an investigation into the beating, alleged torture and killing of an unauthorized Mexican immigrant by U.S. officials after he tried to re-enter California in 2010, the first case by the commission ever to accuse American law enforcement of an extrajudicial slaying.

In a May 10 letter, the commission informed attorneys for the family of Anastasio Hernandez Rojas that the relevant parts of their March 2016 complaint have been forwarded to the Trump administration, which has three months to respond to the allegations of extrajudicial killing, torture and obstruction of justice.

If the administration doesn’t respond, the commission has the power to enter a default judgment on behalf of Hernandez Rojas’ family, according to a press release by Alliance San Diego, an organization representing the relatives. The organization noted that a failure to respond by the U.S. would break with the nation’s decadeslong active engagement with the commission.

The Trump administration can ask any of several different agencies to respond to the complaint, according to Alliance San Diego spokesman Hiram Soto.

U.S. Border Patrol, U.S. Customs and Border Protection and U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents beat and used a stun gun on Hernandez Rojas, 42, a Mexican citizen and father of five who had lived for years in San Diego, after arresting him as he re-entered the U.S. days after being deported in May 2010, according to court documents. Within minutes of being beaten, Hernandez Rojas had stopped breathing. He died the next day. 

"Not only was Anastasio's life taken, but an inadequate investigation was conducted: CBP had control of the crime scene for several hours, evidence was destroyed, eyewitnesses were dispersed,” said Roxanna Altholz, the associate director at the University of California Berkeley Law School's International Human Rights Law Clinic who is also representing Hernandez Rojas’ family. “The U.S. government thought it could sweep this horrific crime under the rug by closing the criminal case. Now, we look to this commission [to] shine the international spotlight on the agency's lack of oversight and accountability and identify the reforms necessary to end impunity for this agency."

Alliance San Diego noted in its press release that a video recorded by an eyewitness made international news and has served as “damning evidence” of CBP’s brutality and that the incident triggered congressional inquiries, an investigation by CBP into the use of force and new policies issued by the agency limiting the use of Tasers, and the practice of federal immigration agents wearing body cameras.

In March, a California federal judge provisionally signed off on an order approving a $1 million settlement between the federal government and Hernandez Rojas’ family.

According to court documents, Hernandez Rojas was initially arrested and detained on May 10, 2010, for allegedly stealing food for his family and was deported two weeks later. He and his brother, Pedro, were arrested on May 28 by immigration agents while re-entering the U.S. near Otay Mesa in San Diego.

After being transported to a screening area, Hernandez Rojas was told by an agent to put his gallon jug of water in the trash, court documents say. As he began pouring the water in the trash can, the Border Patrol agent knocked the jug out of his hands, spun him around and marched him to wall, where the agent kicked his legs apart and struck metal screws implanted in Hernandez Rojas’ ankle in the process, according to court documents.

When Hernandez Rojas complained of pain, the agent pushed him against a wall and handcuffed him, according to court documents. The agent and his partner then reportedly led Hernandez Rojas into the station, where he complained that he had been hit and kicked and wanted medical care. The station supervisor told Hernandez Rojas that none of the agents had hurt his ankle and instead ordered that the agent accused of hitting and kicking him return him to Mexico, court documents say.

Hernandez Rojas was then handcuffed and driven to the San Ysidro border crossing. When they parked, the agents led Hernandez Rojas a few feet from the car and took his handcuffs off. When he supposedly put his hands down instead of behind his head, one of the agents grabbed him from behind and a struggle ensued, according to court documents.

Two other agents ran to the scene and began beating Hernandez Rojas with batons, striking him multiple times, and then threw him to the ground, face down, and handcuffed him, court documents say. Another agent reportedly arrived and pressed down on Hernandez Rojas’ back. He was also hit with a stun gun. Two other agents arrived and grabbed Hernandez Rojas’ legs, twisted them and held him face down, according to court documents.

At one point, one of the agents realized that Hernandez Rojas was not breathing. He was without oxygen for many minutes until paramedics obtained a pulse, by which time he was brain-dead, court documents say. Hernandez Rojas was put on a ventilating machine for a day and a half. He was then unhooked from the machine and died, according to court documents.

His family filed a complaint in California federal court against the federal government in March 2011. The third amended complaint, filed a year later, brought causes of action against the individual defendants including retaliation, excessive force, wrongful death, failure to properly supervise and failure to intervene, as well as the deprivation of the right of familial association. That was the case settled in March.

The U.S. Department of Justice closed its investigation into Hernandez Rojas' death in November 2015 after finding that there wasn't enough evidence to prosecute the agents on federal criminal charges of civil rights violations. 

Representatives for the DOJ declined to comment Friday. The CBP and ICE did not immediately return requests for comment Friday.

Hernandez Rojas is represented by Roxanna Altholz, Amanda Barrow, Jessica Oliva, Kelsey Quigley and Aaron Voit of the UC Berkeley Law School’s International Human Rights Law Clinic and by Andrea Guerrero of Alliance San Diego.

The case is Family Members of Anastasio Hernandez Rojas v. United States, case number not available, before the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights.
