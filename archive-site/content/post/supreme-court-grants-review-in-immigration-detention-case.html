Today, the Supreme Court granted certiorari in Anlbece v. Chavez.  The Solicitor General sought review of a Third Circuit ruling on the question whether an alien who is detained under 8 U.S.C. § 1231 is entitled by statute, after six months of detention, to a bond hearing at which the government must prove to an immigration judge by clear and convincing evidence that the alien is a flight risk or a danger to the community.  The cert petition contends that the Third Circuit's requirement of a bond hearing is inconsistent with the Court's ruling in Jennings v. Rodriguez.  In Jennings, as I previously summarized,

"Justice Samuel Alito wrote for a 5-3 court. (Justice Elena Kagan recused herself, in all likelihood because she was involved in the case while serving as U.S. solicitor general.) Using a textual approach to interpreting the immigration statute, the majority found that nothing in the statute supports the imposition of periodic bond hearings as mandated by the court of appeals. The court held that, because the statute was clear, the 9th Circuit had misapplied the doctrine of constitutional avoidance. Alito emphasized that “a court relying on that canon … must interpret the statute, not rewrite it.”
