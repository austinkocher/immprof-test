The New York Times reports that Alabama Gov. Robert Bentley resigned yesterday after a sex scandal brought him to the brink of impeachment and prompted a series of criminal investigations.

Ellen Brooks, a special prosecutor, said that Bentley quit in connection with a plea agreement on two misdemeanor charges: failing to file a major contribution report and knowingly converting campaign contributions to personal use.  Bentley, a Republican, acknowledged in March 2016 that he had made sexually charged remarks to his senior political adviser, Rebekah Caldwell Mason.

Governor Bentley signed HB 56, a harsh immigration enforcement measure,  into law in 2011.  The U.S. Department of Justice and immigrant and civil rights groups filed successful legal challenges to the law.  Bentley was a supporter of the law.  For analysis of the civil rights impacts of H.B. 56, click here.
