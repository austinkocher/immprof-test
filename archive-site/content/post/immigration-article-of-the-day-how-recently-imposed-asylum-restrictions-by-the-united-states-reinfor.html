How Recently Imposed Asylum Restrictions by the United States Reinforce Migration-Related Disinformation and Undermine International Law by Kimberly Breedon, University of St. Thomas Journal of Law and Public Policy, Forthcoming

Abstract

Disinformation has become a standard weapon in the efforts by authoritarian regimes and their ideological allies to dismantle multiracial, pluralistic, liberal democracy in the United States. Opponents of democracy have increasingly launched—with varying degrees of success—disinformation campaigns designed to weaken the structures and institutions of liberal, democratic governance; unravel international democratic alliances; and undermine international agreements. One particularly effective subject for these malign influence campaigns is disinformation that dehumanizes and demonizes migrants seeking refuge or asylum in other, usually democratic, nations by characterizing migrants as threats to the destination-state population’s health, safety, security, or national or racial identity. This type of migration-related disinformation all too often translates into anti-immigration policies in the states targeted by the disinformation. This paper considers whether the tightening of U.S. asylum law reinforces the harmful, false narratives perpetuated by migration-related disinformation, and enables those narratives to undermine U.S. commitments pertaining to global migration under international law.
