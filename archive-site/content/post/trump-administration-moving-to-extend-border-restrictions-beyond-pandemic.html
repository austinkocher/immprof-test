New York Times reports:

 

The Trump administration is moving to extend its virus border restrictions indefinitely, using the government’s broad public health authorities to severely limit immigration across its land borders until officials decide that there is no more danger of infection to Americans.

The order in question was enacted on March 21 by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. It imposed a 30-day restriction on all nonessential travel into the United States from Mexico and Canada, closing legal points of entry to tourism and immediately returning people who crossed the border illegally to their home countries. It resulted in all but 2 migrants seeking asylum to be turned away from the border.

 

The order was then extended on April 20 for another 30 days. 

 

A draft order being circulated in anticipation of the May 20 expiration date would allow the border to remain closed indefinitely, until CDC Director Dr. Robert R. Redfield decides the virus no longer poses a threat. While the director would review the order every 30-days, the presumption switches to the border remaining closed.

 

"“I am extending the duration of the order until I determine that the danger of further introduction of Covid-19 into the United States has ceased to be a danger to the public health."

 

The CDC has not shared the criteria to guide the border restriction decisions. Some say there is troubling inconsistency between the government continuing to declare a public health threat at the border while lifting state orders to restrict movement, suggesting that the border restrictions are motivated by a desire to restrict immigration more than by public health concerns.
