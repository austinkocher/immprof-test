Recent media reports revealed news of a shocking case where a young mother, Christina Salazar, and her four children—including newborn twins born on U.S. soil—were deported to Mexico and separated from her U.S. citizen husband after missing a court hearing due to an emergency C-section. 

Co-Executive Director Beatriz Lopez issued a statement in response:

“The deportation of Christina Salazar and her four young children, including newborn twins born on U.S. soil, is not just heartbreaking for her family, but also a stark warning of what is to come under Trump's extreme mass deportation agenda that seeks to detain, deport and separate families at unprecedented scale. 

“This case exemplifies a broader agenda that seeks to undermine fundamental rights. The deportation of the U.S.-born infants is a moral and policy failure that should strike fear and outrage in our national conscience. We are deeply concerned that the incoming Trump administration will aggressively seek opportunities similar to the Arellanos case to force the birthright issue into the courts as soon as possible. The ultimate goal, thinly veiled, is to undermine birthright citizenship itself. Press reports have already speculated that such efforts could include directing the Department of State and the Social Security Administration to withhold passport and social security numbers from U.S. born children who can’t establish that one or both parents is a U.S. citizen or has permanent status in the U.S. at the time of birth. This issue demands serious attention from all lawmakers who believe in the Constitution and the sanctity of family.

“We must stand with families like the Arellanos, who seek the chance to live, love, and thrive together in our country. But let’s be clear: this is not just about one family. It’s about challenging a law that is fundamental to who we are as Americans while jeopardizing the lives of tens of millions of mixed-status American families across the country who are now living in fear of family separation, detention and deportation.”
