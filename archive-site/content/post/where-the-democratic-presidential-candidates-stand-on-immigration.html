KQED draws attention to the Democratic presidential candidate stances on immigration, in anticipation of California's Super Tuesday primary vote and the salience of immigration for California voters. Their rankings note significant common ground among the candidates. All of the candidates would:

  • Reverse the Trump administration’s “travel ban” that bars visitors and immigrants from a number of African and Muslim-majority countries;
  • Stop diverting military funds to build a border wall;
  • Reinstate Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals program established under President Obama, and create a path to citizenship for DACA recipients;

Key comparisons on willingness to decriminalize border crossing (Sanders and Warren, but not Biden or Bloomberg), make immigration courts independent (Sanders and Warren, but not Biden or Bloomberg), and abolish ICE/CBP (Sanders only). Their rankings provide another look at the ratings from ImmProf Michael Kagan whose evaluation of the candidates was previously blogged here and has since been updated. 

UPDATE: President Trump's performance on immigration is evaluated by the Washington Times as strong for promises to end DACA, build a border wall, boost detention and reduce asylum. He is considered lacking for failure to enact measures to strengthen American workers against immigrant workers and failure to reduce overall levels of legal migration. RAICES has also put out a report card that ranks Democratic candidates and Trump.
