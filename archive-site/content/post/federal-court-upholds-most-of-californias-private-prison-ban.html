--- title: "Federal Court Upholds Most of California's Private Prison Ban" date: 2020-10-09T02:06:28 url: "/post/federal-court-upholds-most-of-californias-private-prison-ban/" aliases: ["/immigration/2020/10/federal-court-upholds-most-of-californias-private-prison-ban.html"] type: post year: "2020" category: "Current Affairs" category_slug: "current-affairs" byline: "Immigration Prof" ---

Alyssa Aquino for Law360 reports that a federal judge in the Southern District of California upheld most of the state's private prison ban by ruling that it does not conflict with the U.S. government's enforcement authority, but ordered the state to lift the ban on private facilities used by the the U.S. Marshals Services.

In the order, U.S. District Judge Janis Sammartino, appointed by President George W. Bush, rejected the most of the arguments of the U.S. government and private prison operator GEO Group Inc. seeking to strike down Assembly Bill 32, which outlaws private prisons and would lead to the shutdown of several of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement's privately-operated detention centers, including its Adelanto and Mesa Verde facilities.

Judge Sammartino rejected arguments that California had intruded on the U.S. government's immigration authority, saying that federal immigration law does not require ICE to hold immigrants in private detention facilities.

"Because GEO has failed to establish a clear and manifest congressional intent that ICE house immigration detainees in private detention facilities, the court concludes that AB 32 is not obstacle preempted as applied to GEO's contracts with ICE," the court said.
