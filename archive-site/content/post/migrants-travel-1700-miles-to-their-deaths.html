Recently, nineteen migrants were found near the U.S.-Mexico border, shot dead, their bodies burned beyond recognition. As the BBC reports, many hailed from San Marcos, Guatemala, a city more than 1700 miles from where they died.

One of the migrants who died was a talented player on his local soccer team (with a much-valued strong left foot!). Details about the young man--Marvin Tomás, age 22--come from his soccer team's vice-president who talked about the poverty that drove Tomás to the United States: "He would come to football training early, then work all day and study at the university at weekends. In an eight-hour day, he'd earn about six dollars. With that he had to support his mother and two young sisters, plus help out an older sister - a single mother on her own."

Another migrant who died was a family member of the soccer VP who spoke to BBC about Tomás. The migrant, Edgar López y López, age 50, was deported after being caught up in the 2019 raids of chicken plants in Mississippi. He was trying to return stateside to his wife and children.
