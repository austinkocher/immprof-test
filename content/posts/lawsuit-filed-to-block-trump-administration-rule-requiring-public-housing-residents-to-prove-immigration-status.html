From the Los Angeles Times: Public Counsel has sued the federal housing agency to halt a new rule that would require every person living in federal public housing to prove certain legal immigration status in order to remain in their home.

Noncitizens unlawfully in the country and those with certain temporary protections such as recipients of Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA), do not qualify for the federally-funded rent subsidies.

"The rule, which takes effect mid-December, is a change from a decades-long provision that allowed family members with different immigration statuses to live together in public housing, where rents are subsidized by the federal government, or housing paid for with federally-funded vouchers."

According to the Public Counsel press release,

"The lawsuit argues that the rule violates the Administrative Procedure Act, the Fair Housing Act, and the U.S. Constitution, and that it overrides the framework Congress created to keep mixed-status families together. It asks the court to declare the rule unlawful . . . .

The case is People Organized for Westside Renewal (POWER), Union de Vecinos, et al. v. U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, filed in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California. Plaintiffs are represented by Public Counsel, Kendall Brill & Kelly LLP, and Bird Marella LLP."
