Daniel I. Morales & Nicholas De Genova, Toward Border Abolition: Migrant Struggles and the Law (Duke 2026).

Publisher's Description:
Every act of border crossing without state authorization represents an incipient disposition toward borders and immigration controls that must be understood to be objectively abolitionist.  In their active evasions and subversions of border enforcement, illegalized migrants make a priority of their human needs and desires  over and against the legal regimes of states, and thus defy and at least implicitly repudiate the prerogatives and authority of states to police borders and exclude people on the move.  The Introduction to this book situates this abolitionism in practice of migrants and refugees seizing and exercising their freedom of movement within a genealogy of abolitionist thought, from the reflections of W.E.B. Du Bois on the challenges of emancipation in the aftermath of slavery to more contemporary formulations of carceral and penal abolitionism.  Building upon these perspectives, the Introduction engages the rising prominence of abolitionist thought in migrant struggles and activist scholarship on migration.  As a larger conceptual framework for the volume as a whole, the Introduction contends that the seeming dysfunction of immigration law and border regimes in fact exposes that these legal and border enforcement systems are working, rather predictably, to produce the "illegality" and racial subordination of undocumented migrant labor in the service of neoliberal capitalism. This critical perspective has profound implications for legal liberalism and the foundational reformism of the legal profession, which has long assumed that the immigration regimes of states are inherently legitimate, fundamentally well-intentioned, and perfectible — rather than apparatuses of domination.  Within and against the operations of the law, the struggles of migrants to remake their lives signal a diminutive but irrepressible demand for a different world and thus provide a vital inspiration to the critical imagination of activists and scholars devoted to demanding non-reformist reforms that retain border abolition as their horizon.
