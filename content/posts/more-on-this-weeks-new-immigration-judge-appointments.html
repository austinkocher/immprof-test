Following up on my post from yesterday on the new immigration judge appointments to share the above link to the Substack by former Immigration Judge David Koelsch.

In his post on the same topic, he offers important insights on the new crop of judges.

As he highlights, few of the judges appointed have prior experience in immigration law:

In terms of prior employment, 9 of the 32 new IJs were prosecutors with the Office of the Principal Legal Advisor (OPLA), which is part of the U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS); 7 were state or federal prosecutors; 4 came from the military; 2 were in-house counsel for companies; 2 were in private practice (but only one of those practiced immigration law); 2 were state court or administrative judges; and the rest came from the Bureau of Prisons, Customs and Border Protection, the Social Security Administration, and even the Tennessee Valley Authority.
