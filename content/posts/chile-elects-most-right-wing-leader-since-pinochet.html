Immigration worries have been big in Chile for the last few years. Andra B. Chastain for The Conversation analyzes the election by Chilean voters of "the most right-wing presidential candidate since the end of the Augusto Pinochet dictatorship over three and a half decades ago."

In a runoff held this past weekend, conservative José Antonio Kast received over 58% of the vote, while his left-wing opponent, Jeannette Jara, received nearly 42%. According to Professor Chastain, "the primary issues of voter concern during this campaign were crime and immigration."  Sounds like elections in the USA!

Professor Chastain notes that, "as a historian of modern Latin America and Chile, [she]I believe[s] Chile’s election . . . reflects the important local context of years of increasing disenchantment with the political system."
