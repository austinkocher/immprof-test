A new National Academy of Sciences report on the integration of refugees and asylum seekers in the United States concludes that refugees and asylum seekers have little effect on crime and might even improve local economies for everyone.

The report reviewed the latest research and heard from experts in resettlement and local government policy. They conducted their own analyses and compared U.S. policy and outcomes to those in other countries.

The report’s main conclusions:

  • Refugees catch up to American-born citizens socioeconomically. Asylum seekers catch up more slowly and on fewer outcomes.
  • Forced migrants have small impacts on U.S. labor markets, and their financial impacts on governments vary. 
  • Refugee resettlement does not affect crime rates.
  • Forced migrants’ physical and mental health is often worse than that of other migrants, but many of their conditions are treatable with screening and access to health care.
  • In other countries, forced migrants have strengthened labor markets, and being settled in areas with more jobs promotes their integration over time.

In addition to early support, the report recommends more stable and predictable asylum laws.
