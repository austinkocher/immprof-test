A few days ago, the State Department announced a pledge of $1.8 Billion "in life-saving humanitarian funding to OCHA."

Not gonna lie, the announcement initially caught my eye because I thought it was about OCAHO, the Office of the Chief Administrative Hearing Officer--despite the lack of any connection between that entity and the State Department. I didn't realize it was about OCHA, the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs. Though, I mean, basically the same, right?

My (real) dyslexia aside, the announcement is interesting.

For one, the funding is substantial. According to OCHA data, the US previously donated in the millions--the high being 54.7 million in 2023 but only 6.8 million in 2024. So adding $1.8 billion to contributions already made is quite high. And the US has been the number one contributor to OCHA even before increasing its contributions.

It's also noteworthy that OCHA is a UN entity and President Trump has been highly critical of the UN.

Also, OCHA is really doing good – working to help those with the "most severe needs" around the world. OCHA's mission isn't limited to serving those who meet the restrictive definition of "refugee." It's broadly focused on "crisis-affected communities."

Honestly, this is legit great news, though I wonder it fits in with the closure of USAID and the Trump administration's restrictions on refugees. A new angle for your summer research projects, perhaps.
