No More Deaths has released an updated map of migrant deaths for the southern border — data show a significant increase in death rate, especially for deported U.S. residents attempting to return home. From the press release:

"As deaths in ICE detention continue to break records under Trump’s second term, the southern border is becoming deadlier too, according to new data released today by the humanitarian aid organization No More Deaths. The group attributes the new findings to increased border militarization, wall construction, Border Patrol violence, interior ICE enforcement, and mass deportations, including third-country removals and refoulement of asylum seekers.

In an updated version of its comprehensive database and map of recovered migrant remains for the entire US-Mexico border—the first resource of its kind, originally published in 2024—No More Deaths reports that the death rate at the border, especially for US residents attempting to return home, has increased substantially in the past two years, despite a decline in overall crossings (as measured by Border Patrol apprehensions). In Border Patrol’s Tucson Sector, for example—an area that spans most of southern Arizona—the agency reported a total of 463,567 apprehensions for 2024, and No More Deaths documented 157 deaths; in 2025, there were 41,287 apprehensions and 95 documented deaths; and so far in 2026, out of only 18,074 reported apprehensions, there have been 117 reported deaths. Put another way: the mortality rate at the border has risen from 34 deaths per 100,000 apprehensions in 2024, to approximately 647 deaths per 100,000 apprehensions in 2026—a staggering 1,810% increase in the death rate over a period of less than three years.

Summarizing the report’s original findings in 2024, No More Deaths wrote at the time: `With the new administration’s push for mass deportations, we will likely see more of our neighbors killed by violent US border policy.' This prediction, tragically, has proven true: The increase in the death rate since 2024 is especially pronounced for long-time US residents, as the Trump administration continues to fast-track mass deportations from the interior, forcing more and more people to risk their lives crossing an increasingly militarized border to return home to their families in the US.

The new data also show that deaths from border enforcement—defined as in-custody deaths, or deaths caused by falls from the wall, use of force, or pursuit—now account for a larger share of border fatalities when compared to deaths resulting from `prevention through deterrence,' the US border policy that funnels migrants into remote terrain in hopes that the desert will kill them. Prior to 2025, for example, roughly 15% of migrant deaths in the El Paso Sector, which spans all of New Mexico and two counties in West Texas, were caused directly by Border Patrol enforcement; in 2025 and 2026, that percentage had more than doubled, accounting for over one-third of all deaths in the sector."
