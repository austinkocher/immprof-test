We learned yesterday of the loss of a legendary social justice advocate.

Dolores Huerta, who co-founded what became known as the United Farm Workers union to help secure justice for farm workers, has died at age 96. She passed away at home in Bakersfield, California, surrounded by family.

Here is the obituary from Huerta's foundation. It begins:

"Dolores Huerta inspired generations with three words: Sí, se puede'—`Yes, we can.' In her hands, those words became a rallying cry for workers, women, immigrants and people fighting for justice around the world. She gave people the courage to recognize their own power—and showed them how to use it. Through historic labor contracts, groundbreaking legislation and campaigns that helped women claim political leadership, she turned a declaration of possibility into lasting change."

I saw Dolores Huerta a couple months ago at the opening of an exhibit on mass deportations (Help Wanted / Leave Now: “Operation Wetback” & The Revolving Door of Mass Deportation) at the California Museum in Sacramento. She served on the advisory committee for the exhibit and inspired the audience in speaking at the opening. This is her endorsement of the exhibit:

The exhibit tells the story of repeated mass recruitment and betrayal. The United States has a history of enticing people to come here in times of need or crisis and then deporting them in times of political convenience. It’s a constant cycle of hypocrisy, and the story needs to be told: immigrants built the economy in the United States, and people must not forget that.
Dolores Huerta
