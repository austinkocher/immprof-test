Rebecca Carballo for Politico reports that a federal judge threw out a Trump administration rule yesterday that would have restricted the time foreign students are allowed to stay in the United States. Here is the ruling and background on the case.

The Department of Homeland Security had issued a final rule to replace the “duration of status” system, which allowed most student visa holders to remain in the country as long as they maintain lawful status and continue making normal progress in their programs.

The Trump administration's rule would have capped the amount of time holders of F and J visas could stay in the country to four years, regardless of how long a degree or program takes. Students, researchers and professors who needed more time would have had to apply for extensions under the new rule.

"A coalition of education advocacy groups sued over the rule in August, arguing it was arbitrary and would discourage foreign students from studying in the U.S.

The regulation was one in a series of moves that the Trump administration has taken to limit the number of foreign students studying in the United States. Administration officials argue that the stricter policies are designed to discourage visa fraud and that Americans should not have to compete against international students for work."
