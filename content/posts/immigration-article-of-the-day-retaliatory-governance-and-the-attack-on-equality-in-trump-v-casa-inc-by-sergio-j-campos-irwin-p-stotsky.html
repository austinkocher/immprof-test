Retaliatory Governance and the Attack on Equality in Trump v. CASA, Inc. by Sergio J. Campos & Irwin P. Stotsky, American Journal of Law and Equality, volume 6, 2026

Abstract

In Trump v. CASA, the Supreme Court limited the availability of universal injunctive relief, concluding that Congress did not grant federal courts sufficient equitable power to issue such relief except in limited circumstances like the class action. The Court did so in the context of a constitutional challenge to an executive order abolishing the birthright citizenship protected by the Fourteenth Amendment. And the President issued that executive order in the context of what we call retaliatory governance-a mode of governance adopted by the current administration which uses the power of the executive to punish its enemies. 

In this Article we examine the implications of the Court's decision on three aspects of equality: (1) class-action practice and the cohesiveness of the parties, (2) the equitable authority of federal courts, and (3) the rule of law-particularly the guarantee of equality-in our constitutional system. In discussing the decision's attack on equality, we discuss the relationship of the Court's actions, both in this case and more generally, to the retaliatory governance of the Trump administration.
