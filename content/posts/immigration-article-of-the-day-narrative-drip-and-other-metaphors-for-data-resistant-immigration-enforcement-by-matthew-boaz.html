Narrative Drip And Other Metaphors For Data-Resistant Immigration Enforcement by Matthew Boaz

Abstract
In the United States, 2025 has been besieged by rapid, broadscale, and far-reaching actions by the executive branch. While the entire federal government and reliant agencies have been reeling from mass firings, withholding of congressionally appropriated funds, and a creeping sense of precarity, nowhere has the election of Donald Trump to a second term created more chaos than in the field of immigration law and policy. To render a full accounting of the vast changes that have taken place would be ill-advised, since each day brings news of unforeseen terrors being inflicted on the noncitizens who reside in the United States and their families.

The primary through line of these actions is the articulation of most migration into the United States as being a “foreign threat.” The most recently issued executive actions focus on invasion, refer to terrorism, and describe antagonistic actors who have the intent of harming specific states or the country at large. The effect has been to reaffirm to the public these actions are targeted at “criminals,” “gang members,” and “the worst of the worst,” despite the reality that a substantial proportion of those who are affected have no criminal history and have abided by lawful pathways to temporary status in the United States provided by prior administrations. The actions taken by the Trump administration are inarticulate, uncareful, and designed to inflict maximum fear among those who lack citizenship in the United States. Even those who possess citizenship, if born to noncitizen or non-legal permanent resident parents, have been the target of the administration’s nefarious ministrations.

To support these radical actions, spokespeople for the White House have employed a tool I will term here as “narrative drip” that works to create a broad funnel through which regular untruths, speculations, and defamatory statements about immigrants can flow as a collective.
