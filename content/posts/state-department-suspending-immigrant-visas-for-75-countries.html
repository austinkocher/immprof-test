Matthew Lee for the Associated Press reports on a jarring development. "The State Department said [today] it will suspend the processing of immigrant visas for citizens of 75 countries, including Afghanistan, Iran, Russia and Somalia, whose nationals the Trump administration has deemed likely to require public assistance while living in the United States."

The State Department said it had instructed consular officers to halt immigrant visa applications from the 75 countries consistent with an order issued in November that tightened rules around prospective immigrants likely to become "public charges" in the United States. The suspension of immigrant visas for citizens of these nations apparently will end individualized review of their visa applications to determine whether the individual will likely become a public charge.

The suspension begins January 21 and will not apply to applicants seeking nonimmigrant visas, such as tourist or business visas. Demand for nonimmigrant visas is expected to rise with the upcoming 2026 World Cup and 2028 Olympics in the United States.
