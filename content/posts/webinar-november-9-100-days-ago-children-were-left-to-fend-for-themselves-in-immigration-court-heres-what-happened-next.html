100 Days Ago, Children Were Left to Fend for Themselves in Immigration Court. Here’s What Happened Next.

Date & Time: Nov 9, 2026 10:00 AM in Pacific Time (US and Canada)

Description

On July 31, 2026, the federal contract that paid for lawyers for unaccompanied children in immigration court ended. Monday, November 9 is day 100. Since then, Acacia Center for Justice's court observers have documented children's cases moving at unprecedented speed, appeals denied within days, children assigned to judges in other states on remote dockets, and pressure to accept voluntary departure.

On Monday, November 9 at 1:00 pm Eastern, join Austin Kocher for a live conversation with Dr. Marsha Griffin of Community for Children, Katie Fleming of Acacia Center for Justice and Neha Desai of the National Center for Youth Law on what the court observers have seen and what the children's cases look like 100 days on. Austin will close with what Acacia's data shows about how fast these cases are being decided.

Marsha Griffin, MD, is a co-founder, President and CEO of Community for Children, a founding member of the American Academy of Pediatrics Council on Immigrant Child and Family Health, co-author of the AAP Policy Statement "Detention of Immigrant Children," and a trustee of the Acacia Center for Justice.

Katie Fleming is the Director of Public Education and Engagement at the Acacia Center for Justice, oversees its Witness for Justice court observation initiative, and is an attorney with nearly 15 years of experience in immigration law.

Neha Desai is the Managing Director of Children's Human Rights and Dignity at the National Center for Youth Law and has served as counsel on multiple federal class actions representing children detained in federal immigration custody.
