The Baseline



A new monthly column from Austin Kocher that explores the impact of rising immigration enforcement. 

By Austin Kocher


Illustration: Victor Bizar Gomez


New York City has long been a place where immigrants from all over the world seek a better life for themselves. For immigrants fleeing persecution, the city is also a place of hopeful refuge where they live with their future on hold until their humanitarian case is decided by a judge.

Historically, New York's immigration courts have stood out in the United States as a place where asylum seekers could get a fair hearing in a system plagued by procedural and substantive injustices. Between 2001 and 2024, when asylum was all but impossible at other major courts in Miami, Atlanta, and Houston — where judges denied 79%, 86%, and 88% of asylum applications, respectively — New York immigration judges denied just 31% of all asylum applications.

A grant means a judge has found that the person qualifies as a refugee and may stay in the United States, with a path to permanent residence; a denial usually ends with an order of deportation. A small share of people denied asylum receive a narrower form of protection, such as withholding of removal, which bars their deportation to the country they fled but offers no path to permanent status.

But then, something strange happened in December 2025: New York City's immigration courts ceased to be special. In that month, New York's judges granted just 10% of asylum applications, the same percentage that was granted in all other immigration courts across the country. The trend has continued through the end of fiscal year 2026 in September, as New York City courts denied 92% of the asylum applications for year, mirroring the rest of the courts in the country.

The data on this momentous surge in asylum denial rates illustrates how distinctive New York City had been for so long, and how powerless any single immigration court is to resist the barrage of changes implemented by an aggressively deportation-obsessed administration.
