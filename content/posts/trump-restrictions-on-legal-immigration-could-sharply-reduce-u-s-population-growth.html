America's national conversation about immigration has centered recently on dramatic enforcement operations in communities across the country. But a parallel story is unfolding with less fanfare, one that may have a lasting impact on the country's demographic future and economic prospects.

A new Migration Policy Institute (MPI) short read (and here) details the restrictions on legal immigration undertaken so far during the second Trump term. These include travel bans affecting nationals of 39 countries, a pause on permanent visa issuance for 75 countries, a $100,000 fee for employers sponsoring high-skilled H-1B workers, and new vetting requirements that have led to a sizable drop in student visa issuance.

The stakes extend well beyond the immigration system itself. With U.S. birth rates at historic lows, immigration now accounts for the vast majority of U.S. population growth. The country's natural population increase (births minus deaths) stands at roughly 500,000 per year and is falling toward zero. A substantial drop in legal immigration could tip the United States into outright population decline, a development last seen in 1918, during the overlap of World War I and a pandemic.

The analysis makes clear that narrowing the legal immigration pipeline, incrementally and across multiple visa categories, carries real implications for the country's long-term growth and competitiveness.
